### This Week's Story: The Lenders Joined, Just as the Curve Turned on Them

XLF rose **2.66% to $58.68**, its sixth straight weekly gain, against SPY +2.6% and XLK +3.4%. Last week it was a Berkshire-and-networks fund; this week sixteen of the seventeen large names rose.

**Catalyst 1 — Berkshire's quarter (Sat Nov 7):** Operating earnings fell 5.8% to $12.7B, with insurance underwriting down 43%, but buybacks more than doubled to $9.6B. The stock rose 0.8% Monday and kept going to a two-year closing high Friday.

**Catalyst 2 — The dealers bounce (Mon Nov 9):** XLF +1.5% on the week's heaviest volume, Goldman +4.7%. Last Thursday's 71.5M-share selloff did not follow through. Through the week, the Anthropic IPO, with reports of a valuation above $1.5T and a raise over $100B, became the biggest underwriting story of the year.

**Catalyst 3 — Hot CPI (Tue Nov 10):** Core CPI +0.3% against 0.2%, a third firm month. The 2-year rose 17bp that day and 37bp on the week, to 4.88%. BofA said 50bp in December was now possible and then forecast a January hike as well. 2s10s flattened to +27bp.

**Catalyst 4 — Rates higher, banks higher anyway (Wed–Fri):** The 10-year closed at 5.15% and the 30-year auction cleared at 5.441%. The money centers held their gains; the regionals and KRE did not participate (+0.75%).

**The Bottom Line:** For two weeks the worry was that the fund's gains rested on Berkshire and two card networks. This week the lenders carried their share. But the curve that was the lenders' tailwind all autumn flattened 8bp in a week on a hike repricing, and the small banks most exposed to deposit costs were flat to down. The dealers have the IPO calendar; the regionals have the 2-year.

### ⚠️ The Wild Card
**A hiking cycle with a positive curve.** Every bank model on this page assumed rates rise because growth is strong, which is what the curve said all autumn. This week the front end moved twice as fast as the long end for an inflation reason. If the Fed hikes twice into a 5% 10-year, the curve can stay positive and still squeeze the lenders, because the 2-year is what deposits price off.
