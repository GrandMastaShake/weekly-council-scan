RTX fell -5.16% despite MarketBeat coverage highlighting the stock is still up 30.7%+ over the past 52 weeks and carries a "Moderate Buy" consensus with a $232 target — Navy-modernization tailwinds remain intact but the group got swept up in the broader risk-off. **The Week's Three Stories:**

**Treasury Yields Broke Out — And That Broke the Sector's Multiples:** The 10-year touched 4.814%, its highest level since November 2023, before easing on later Fed commentary that split policymakers between "stronger economy" and "Wall Street frets" camps. **The August ISM Confirmed the Flash-PMI Warning, But Barely:** Manufacturing PMI printed 54.6, down 1 point from July's near-four-year high of 55.6 and below the 55.2 consensus. New orders fell sharply (-3.0 pts to 53.7) and backlogs declined (-3.2 pts to 51.8), while the Prices Index held at an elevated 71.1 for a 23rd straight month of input-cost increases. This is still expansion — an eighth consecutive month above 50 — but it is the first genuine loss-of-momentum reading since the advance began, and it landed the same week yields spiked. The two data points reinforce each other: input costs remain high, demand is softening at the margin, and the market repriced accordingly.

**Deere Proved the Cycle Thesis Can Still Work — On the Right Name:** While the sector average fell, Deere hit an all-time high above $700, up 10% on the week alone, on growing sell-side conviction that its August 20 guidance raise marks the start of an agricultural-equipment recovery, not a one-off beat. Eaton's data-center capex story similarly strengthened independent of the macro. The lesson for the Council: company-specific catalysts (a genuine guidance raise, a genuine capacity expansion tied to AI demand) can still overpower a hostile macro tape — but they are the exception this week, not the rule.

## THE WEEKLY NARRATIVE — What's Actually Moving XLI

> *This section is rewritten weekly by the Saturday Research Crew. It captures the story beneath the headlines.*

### This Week's Story: Yields, Not Tariffs, Ran the Table

The narrative shifted this week. Everything the Council had been tracking on the trade front — Canada's retaliation, the September 8 collection date, the escalation ladder — arrived exactly on schedule and mattered less than a single macro number: the 10-year Treasury touching 4.814%, its highest level since November 2023. For a sector where the most-loved names trade at 25-41x earnings, that kind of move in the discount rate does real damage across the board, and it did: XLI fell -2.76% to $175.27, its third straight down week, and a Perplexity Finance market note flagged that roughly 45% of S&P 500 industrial stocks are now in bear-market territory — the worst breadth reading since the 2025 Liberation Day selloff.

The August ISM print added fuel without being a disaster on its own. Manufacturing PMI came in at 54.6, a full point below July's near-four-year high of 55.6 and short of the 55.2 consensus — still an eighth straight month of expansion, but the first genuine loss of momentum in new orders and backlogs since this advance began, with input costs (Prices Index 71.1) staying stubbornly elevated. Combined with the Canada counter-tariffs taking effect on schedule September 8, the macro backdrop gave the market every reason to sell first and sort out fundamentals later.

And the fundamentals mostly held up fine underneath the selling. Lockheed Martin fell nearly 7% on the week even as it beat Q2 estimates on both lines and got a price-target hike from Citigroup to $691 — that gap between the stock and the story is the sharpest in the index. RTX told the same tale at a smaller scale. Union Pacific dropped despite zero deterioration in the UP-NS merger review, which is advancing on schedule toward a Q3/Q4 2027 close. The exceptions were real, not just relative: Deere posted its best week of the year, hitting an all-time high above $700 as the market grows convinced its guidance raise marks an actual ag-cycle turn, and Eaton's AI-data-center power story got structurally stronger with a $242M capacity expansion and a new joint reference design with Trane Technologies.

**The Bottom Line:** This is a rates-driven de-rating layered on top of a still-live trade war, not a fundamentals problem — the two clearest exceptions (DE, ETN) both have company-specific catalysts that outran the macro. Watch the 10-year into the September 15-16 FOMC; if it keeps climbing, the sector has further to fall regardless of earnings quality. If it stabilizes, this week priced in more fear than the underlying data supports.

It isn't anymore: a move to a near-three-year yield high did more damage to sector multiples in five trading days than three weeks of tariff headlines combined.
