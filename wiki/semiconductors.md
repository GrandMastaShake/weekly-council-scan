## THE WEEKLY NARRATIVE — What's Actually Moving SMH

> *This section is rewritten weekly by the Saturday Research Crew. It captures the story beneath the headlines.*

### This Week's Story: The Suppliers Beat, the Funding Broke

Last week the market asked whether OpenAI could pay for its chips. This week it asked whether anyone downstream of the hyperscalers could, and decided not to wait for the answer.

**Act I — The Casino.** Monday, with the bond market closed for Columbus Day, Broadcom's CEO told a Singapore conference that many AI investors are effectively in a casino and that capital, not chips, is the next constraint. Microsoft said it would scale its own Maia chips to multi-gigawatt deployments. China's CXMT said it would ship a denser server-DRAM chip by year-end. SOX fell 2.7%, SMH 2.05% to $590.99, through last week's $600.32 line on day one. Tuesday CoreWeave's 2032 bonds traded near 15% and AMAT fell nearly 7%; JPMorgan missed on EPS while Goldman beat.

**Act II — The Prints.** Wednesday's September CPI came in at +0.6% headline (3.7% Y/Y) and **+0.3% core against +0.2%**. October hike odds jumped to about 50%. The 10-year spiked to 5.33% and then closed lower. ASML reported a record quarter before the open. CoreWeave fell 15.5%, Oracle 8.8%, and SOX 4.3% for a sixth straight loss; SMH -3.98% to $564.74, through the 50D. Thursday TSMC beat and raised, PPI printed +1.3%, retail sales +0.8%, and the 10-year fell 10bp to 5.089%. SMH still fell 1.0%.

**Act III — The Flush.** Friday an unconfirmed rumor of an AI hedge-fund blowup sent VIX to 22.31. Warsh, in Bangkok, gave no signal on an October hike. WTI's front month settled above $100. SMH fell 3.41% to $539.79 on 18.4M shares, the heaviest volume of the week, and SOX posted its eighth straight down day, the longest streak since 2008.

**The Bottom Line.** Every fundamental data point from the supply side was positive: TSMC's Q4 guide implies +14% Q/Q, ASML said it cannot meet 2027–28 demand, memory supply is sold out through 2027. The stocks fell 8–20%. When good supplier news meets falling prices, the market is telling you it does not trust the demand curve's financing. Next week brings the first answer from a buyer: **Meta and Tesla Wednesday, with Lam Research and Texas Instruments the same evening.** Meta's capex guide matters more to SMH than anything TSMC said.
