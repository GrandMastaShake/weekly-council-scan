**Sector Split:** SMH is dominated by the AI duo — NVDA (~19%) and TSM (~12%) — plus the networking king AVGO (~8%). The rest is a mix of:
- **AI/GPU:** NVDA, AMD (the compute engines)
- **Foundry:** TSM (the manufacturer everyone depends on)
- **Networking/Custom Silicon:** AVGO (the connective tissue)
- **Lithography:** ASML (the monopoly no one can replicate)
- **Memory:** MU, Hynix, Samsung (the volatile commodity players — now supercharged by HBM)
- **Equipment:** LRCX, AMAT, KLAC (the picks and shovels)
- **Legacy x86:** INTC (the turnaround story that isn't turning)
- **Mobile:** QCOM (the 5G/modem leader)

**The NVDA Story:** NVIDIA at ~$223.96, up ~11.6% on the week. NVDA is **~5% below its 52W high** ($236.54) and within striking distance of new highs. The company remains the most important stock in the market, with $1 trillion+ in confirmed purchase commitments through 2027. The H100/H200 chips are sold out. The Blackwell architecture (B100/B200) is the next growth wave. Samsung is now pitching 5nm as HBM4, and Nvidia orders have pushed Samsung's 4nm lines to full capacity through 2027. The stock at 34.2x P/E is not cheap, but it is no longer absurd. The custom silicon risk is growing — Google's TPU v6, Amazon's Trainium 3, Meta's MTIA, and now **Anthropic's in-house chip design team** are all gaining traction. But NVDA's 11.6% weekly rally shows the market still believes. Any disappointment in the August 26 earnings report will be punished.

**The TSM Story:** Taiwan Semiconductor at ~$420.04, up 3.9% on the week. TSM's Q2 earnings two weeks ago — record profit up ~77% YoY — are now being repriced positively as the macro backdrop shifts. TSM raised its 2026 capex guidance to $60–64 billion and pledged another $100 billion to its Arizona campus, pushing total US investment to $265 billion. The company is expanding CoW (chip-on-wafer) outsourcing to OSATs amid an AI packaging bottleneck — a sign that even TSM cannot build capacity fast enough. TSM is the foundry monopoly — it manufactures ~90% of the world's advanced chips. At 36.9x P/E, TSM is not cheap, but it is the most indispensable company in the sector.

**The ASML Story:** ASML at ~$1,741.00, up 6.9% on the week. ASML reported two weeks ago and said it's adding **30% more capacity in both 2027 and 2028** to meet insatiable demand for EUV lithography. The company is targeting 60+ EUV shipments in 2026 — a ~25% increase over 2025. Like TSM, the stock is now being repriced higher as the macro dust settles. ASML's monopoly on advanced lithography means every leading-edge chip — NVIDIA's, AMD's, Apple's — requires ASML machines. The backlog extends to 2027+. At 59.2x P/E, ASML is expensive, but it is the most defensible monopoly in the sector.

**The INTC Story:** Intel at ~$101.65, up 12.7% on the week. This is the Intel paradox — the company's turnaround under Pat Gelsinger is still bleeding cash, the foundry business is losing billions per quarter, and the stock moves like a meme. The 12.7% weekly rally had nothing to do with fundamentals and everything to do with beta — INTC has a 2.24 beta and trades like a leveraged semiconductor proxy. The stock at 49.4x P/E is expensive for a company in structural decline. Is this a value trap or a deep-value turnaround? The market is voting: momentum play, not value.

**The MU Story:** Micron at ~$877.57, up 6.6% on the week. MU is the memory cycle in stock form, and that cycle remains parabolic. HBM (high-bandwidth memory) prices continue to surge. Samsung just became the most profitable company on earth — Q2 profit of $58.5B, edging past NVIDIA's $53B — with 94–96% of that coming from AI memory. SK Hynix has sold out its entire 2026 HBM output. A new development: **DRAM price surge is lifting Samsung and Micron as SK Hynix loses share** — Hynix's singular focus on HBM has slowed its traditional DRAM expansion, creating an opening. Micron is caught between the AI memory super-boom and legacy DRAM/NAND pricing pressure. At ~19.8x P/E, the valuation is actually reasonable for the earnings momentum. Western Digital and Sandisk both reported record quarters on AI-fueled NAND shortage. The memory cycle is not crashing — it is evolving.

**Cecil Fundamental Read:** QCOM at 19.2x P/E and 2.19% yield is still the only mega-cap name that fits Cecil's criteria. Qualcomm is a cash machine — 5G modem royalties, smartphone chip sales, automotive/IoT diversification. The dividend is covered. The balance sheet is fortress-like. But growth is slowing — smartphone sales are flat. NVDA at 34.2x P/E is a growth stock, not a value play. TSM at 36.9x P/E is a monopoly with geopolitical risk. MU at ~19.8x P/E is a commodity cyclical — but at a reasonable multiple for once. INTC at 49.4x P/E is a disaster. The semiconductor sector is Marky's turf, not Cecil's.

Semiconductors had a monster week. SMH surged from $540.53 to $582.70 — a 7.8% weekly gain that erased much of the 19.5% correction from June highs. The PHLX Semiconductor Index bounced hard. For growth stocks, this is oxygen.

**The Alphabet Capex Confirmation.**

Tuesday after the close, Alphabet reported Q2 2026 results that mattered more than almost any macro print. Revenue: $119.8B (+24% YoY). Google Cloud: **+82% YoY growth**. Capex growth: **+26% QoQ**. The message was unmistakable: the AI infrastructure buildout is accelerating, not slowing. Meta's "compute glut" narrative from two weeks ago? Alphabet's numbers contradicted it. The AI capex cycle is alive and well, and the semiconductor sector is its primary beneficiary.

**The Samsung HBM4 Push.**

Samsung's foundry arm is stepping up sales of its 5nm process as its more advanced 4nm capacity remains fully booked through 2027, driven by HBM4 base-die production and Nvidia inference-chip orders. This is the demand confirmation the sector needed after Meta's "renting spare compute" story spooked the tape two weeks ago. Nvidia's orders are not slowing — they are accelerating into 2027.

**The Memory Euphoria Continues.**

Micron at ~$877.57 is up 6.6% on the week. The memory cycle is not crashing — it is evolving. Samsung became the most profitable company on earth in Q2. SK Hynix has sold out its entire 2026 HBM output. But a new dynamic emerged: DRAM price surge is lifting Samsung and Micron as SK Hynix loses traditional DRAM share due to its singular HBM focus. Western Digital reported record results on AI-fueled NAND shortage. Sandisk's data-center pivot is paying off. The memory sub-sector is the most volatile in SMH — and this week, the volatility was to the upside.

**The Anthropic Chip Scare (Long-Term).**

Anthropic is building an in-house team to design custom chips for its Claude AI models. This follows Google's TPU v6, Amazon's Trainium 3, and Meta's MTIA. The custom silicon trend is accelerating. For NVDA, this is the existential long-term risk: if AI labs design their own training chips, NVDA's growth rate compresses. But this is a 2027–2028 story. For 2026, NVDA's H100/H200/Blackwell roadmap has no competition.

**The China/Taiwan Backdrop.**

TSMC's $265 billion Arizona commitment — adding $100 billion to its previous pledge — is the most explicit geopolitical hedging move in the sector's history. The company knows that Taiwan Strait risk is the one factor no model can price. By building a parallel supply chain in the US, Japan, and Germany, TSMC is attempting to make itself "uninvadable" — because the world would still have Arizona fabs. Whether this works is the century's most important industrial question.

**The Bottom Line:** Semiconductors experienced a **macro-driven reversal**. The NFP shock repriced Fed expectations dovishly. The Alphabet capex confirmation reaffirmed the AI infrastructure thesis. The memory cycle evolved rather than crashed. SMH is +7.8% on the week, testing the 50D MA at $595. The correction from June highs is not definitively over — a hot CPI print next week (Aug 12) could reverse the rate narrative — but the sector has found a bid. The question for next week: does SMH clear $595, or does it roll over at resistance?

But growth is slowing. Rule 3 applies: a great company at the wrong point in the cycle is still a bad investment. I am watching from a safe distance. The garden does not grow silicon wafers."*
