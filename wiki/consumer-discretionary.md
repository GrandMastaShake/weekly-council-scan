### This Week's Story: Rates Climbed, Lululemon Cratered, and Tesla's Robotaxi Reveal Fell Flat

The week started where the last one left off — a hawkish Fed chair and a market still digesting what a live September hike would mean for a sector full of expensive multiples. XLY drifted lower through the early sessions as the 10-year Treasury pushed steadily toward its year's highs.

Wednesday and Thursday brought Tesla's Cybercab hype cycle: the stock popped 5-7% into Thursday's invite-only Austin launch event, propelled by anticipation that management would finally show a credible path to robotaxi scale. It didn't. The event wasn't livestreamed, Elon Musk didn't appear, and the company disclosed only 45 registered Cybercabs against a mostly-Model-Y robotaxi fleet — thin enough that the NHTSA opened a safety-standards audit query into the pedal-less, steering-wheel-less vehicle within hours. Friday, TSLA gave back the entire pop and then some, falling -5.92% on the day even though the stock still finished the week positive thanks to Wednesday/Thursday's larger gains.

Friday was also Lululemon's day of reckoning. The company beat on adjusted EPS ($2.92, though $0.86 of that came from a one-time tariff refund) but missed on revenue and posted a 9-10% comparable-sales decline, with Americas comps down 12%. Management cut full-year guidance for the second time in 2026, and the stock collapsed roughly 17-18% to an eight-year low below $100 — its worst single-day move in years, and a sympathetic drag on the rest of athletic apparel and footwear.

The same Friday, the August jobs report landed with a real surprise: +162,000 payrolls against a consensus near 53-56K, with unemployment steady at 4.1%. Good news for the labor market, bad news for rate-cut hopes — the 10-year pushed to roughly 4.78-4.80% by the end of the week, and September hike odds jumped from around 49% to north of 60%.

**The Bottom Line:** XLY fell -1.78% on the week, underperforming a nearly-flat SPY, and closed below both its 50-day and 200-day moving averages for the first time this cycle — a genuine technical breakdown, not just noise. Dollar General's continued beat-and-raise momentum (+8.1%) and Ford's tariff-relief-adjacent bounce (+5.0%) were the lone bright spots in an otherwise broadly red tape. Next week's AEO earnings (Sept 9) and the run-up to the September 15-16 FOMC decision are the two events that will determine whether this was a one-week flush or the start of a deeper leg down.
