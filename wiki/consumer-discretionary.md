### This Week's Story: The Tesla Earnings Implosion

Monday and Tuesday were quiet. XLY drifted lower in a -1% range, digesting the prior week's Iran-driven risk-off. Wednesday, TSLA reported Q2 earnings after the close. The numbers were bad: **33 cents adjusted EPS vs 55 cents expected** (a 40% miss), revenue **down 26% year-over-year** to $28.24B, and capital expenditures **surging 142% to $5.79B** as Tesla pours money into Cybercab, Semi, and Optimus production lines. The stock, already down -1% during regular trading Wednesday, fell another -3% after hours — and that was just the opening act.

Thursday was the bloodbath. TSLA gapped down and kept falling, eventually closing near $316 (down ~12% from Wednesday's close). The contagion spread instantly: AMZN — the sector's 22% weight and the next earnings report on the calendar — was dragged down -4% in sympathy. XLY itself fell **-4.62% in a single session**, its worst day since March. Volume exploded to 12.65M shares. Every sub-sector participated: home improvement (HD -2.6% Thursday, LOW -2.4%), restaurants (MCD -1.1%, SBUX -2.0%), media (DIS -3.2%, CMCSA -4.8%), discount retail (DG -5.1%), and apparel (NKE -4.3%, LULU -3.8%). The only green spot was travel: MAR held flat as summer travel demand proved inelastic.

Friday brought a bounce — but it was breadth without conviction. Thirteen of fifteen tracked names closed green, led by LULU (+3.30%), LOW (+2.83%), and MAR (+2.73%). But TSLA kept falling (-2.08%) and AMZN couldn't get off the mat (-0.66%). XLY's +0.60% Friday bounce on 8.2M volume barely retraced a sixth of Thursday's range. The weekly close at $109.41 — below $110, below both MAs, and -5.22% on the week — is the weakest weekly print since March.

**The Bottom Line:** This was not a macro-driven selloff. The consumer data didn't collapse. Oil didn't spike. The Fed didn't surprise. This was a **concentrated, earnings-driven liquidation** in the sector's second-largest position, with contagion fear ahead of AMZN's July 30 report keeping the bid absent. The fundamental consumer floor is still there — five straight months of retail sales gains, falling delinquencies, surging housing starts. But the technical floor just broke. XLY at $109.41 is in no-man's-land between the 52W low ($105.19) and the gap at $113.35. AMZN on July 30 decides which side of that range we test first.
