## THE WEEKLY NARRATIVE — What's Actually Moving XLE

> *This section is rewritten weekly by the Saturday Research Crew. It captures the story beneath the headlines.*

### Week of November 16 – 20, 2026: The Bounce Is Taken Back

XLE fell **6.9% to $60.51**, its lowest close since August 10. It fell all five days and erased last week's 5.4% gain. The S&P 500 fell 8.6%.

**Oil fell first.** Crude dropped more than 3% on Monday on reports that oil exports from the Middle East were recovering. On Wednesday the U.S. government's weekly report showed crude inventories up 3.4 million barrels, when analysts had expected almost no change, and diesel-type fuel inventories also rose. Oil fell about 2% more. Energy stocks fell 3.3% that day on the heaviest trading of the week. For the week, U.S. crude fell roughly $4 a barrel to about $84.

**A note on the numbers.** Futures contracts expire monthly, and several rolled to the next month this week. Price feeds that follow "the front contract" show oil down about 6% and natural gas up 17%. Comparing the same contract month, oil fell 4% to 5% and gas rose 5% to 7%.

**Refiners lost the most.** The profit margin on making diesel narrowed by about $9 a barrel. Valero fell 12%, Marathon Petroleum 11% and Phillips 66 9%, more than giving back last week's gains.

**Then the market fell.** On Thursday and Friday oil steadied, but stocks everywhere dropped after an accounting probe of Nvidia was reported. Within energy, the companies that depend on customers' spending plans fell hardest: SLB lost 11% for the week and Baker Hughes 9%. Exxon and Chevron rose slightly on Friday.

**Iran.** Nothing decisive. Talks continue with mixed signals, and Iran's Revolutionary Guard again said tankers had struck mines in the Strait of Hormuz, which Reuters could not confirm.

**Small caps.** Comstock Resources, a natural gas producer, fell 22% to a two-year low even though gas prices rose. No explanation was found.

**The Bottom Line:** The fund is just above its 200-day average. OPEC+ meets on November 29 to set 2027 production limits, with oil inventories rising and Gulf supply returning.
