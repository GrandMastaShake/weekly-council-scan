### This Week's Story: Services Join the Party

Last week the energy story was a broadening rally: every sub-sector except services caught the bid. This week the waiting ended for the last holdout. **SLB reported Q2 earnings on Thursday and delivered a blowout** — international revenue surged, margins expanded, and management guided higher on E&P capex. The stock responded with its biggest single-day gain in years: **+11.0%**, closing at $52.42. For the first time since the Iran escalation began, all three energy sub-sectors — E&P, refining, and services — are participating.

WTI crude settled at **$89.31**, up roughly **$6.82 a barrel (+8.3%)** — its second consecutive week of massive gains. Brent was the bigger story, touching **$100.91 intraday on Thursday** before fading to $96.78 by Friday's close. The catalyst remained kinetic: U.S. strikes on military targets near Iran's southern coast entered a second consecutive week; Iran answered with missiles and drones aimed at U.S. bases in Jordan and the Gulf. Reports that Tehran instructed the Houthis to prepare closure drills for the **Bab el-Mandeb Strait** kept traders on edge. With the Strait of Hormuz already seeing disrupted flows, the nightmare scenario of *both* chokepoints compromised is now being actively gamed by risk models.

**The sector rotation was the real story.** Refiners — last week's leaders — took a well-deserved breather: MPC -0.97%, VLO -0.90%, PSX -0.10%. They remain near their 52-week highs, but the momentum pendulum swung to services. E&P names were mixed: EOG +0.62% (approaching its 52-week high), XOM and CVX essentially flat, OXY -0.52%. The integrateds are acting like bond proxies — they rise with oil, but not explosively.

**The supply side offered no relief.** OPEC+ approved another +188k bpd for August, but with Hormuz flows impaired those are paper barrels. Russia is already ~600k bpd below its quota thanks to drone attacks on its infrastructure. And the SPR — 360M barrels, a 40-year low — is a buyer of oil (refill demand ~506k bpd by Q4), not a seller.

**The Bottom Line:** The market is no longer asking "is there a war premium?" — it's asking "how high?" The answer this week was $100 on Brent, even if only intraday. What the war giveth in two weeks, peace can taketh away in a session. RSI-14 at 86 on XLE is a flashing yellow light: the sector is overbought, and overbought sectors with geopolitical catalysts can unwind violently.

- China stimulus drives demand above 105M bpd → oil shortage narrative returns
- OPEC+ extends cuts through 2027 → sustained $80+ floor

### 🔴 Bear Case
- Iran de-escalation → WTI drops to $72–$78 → E&P margins compress, sector-wide selloff
- Global recession → demand drops to 101M bpd → oil glut narrative returns
- Fed hikes in September → recession risk → oil demand destruction
- U.S. shale production surges above 14M bpd → supply overwhelms OPEC+ cuts
- Refining margins peak → MPC/VLO/PSX give back gains

### ⚠️ The Wild Card
The U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve is at **360M barrels — the lowest since 1983**. If the administration announces a refill program (buying oil at $70–$75 to rebuild the SPR), that creates a massive price floor. If they do not refill and a supply shock hits, there is no buffer. The SPR policy is the most underpriced factor in the energy market right now.
