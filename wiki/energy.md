## THE WEEKLY NARRATIVE — What's Actually Moving XLE

> *This section is rewritten weekly by the Saturday Research Crew. It captures the story beneath the headlines.*

### Week of November 23 – 27, 2026: Saudi Refineries Under Fire, U.S. Oil Shrugs

XLE rose **1.9% to $61.67** in a four-day week (Thanksgiving closed Thursday; Friday was a half session). The S&P 500 rose 5.6%.

**Attacks in Saudi Arabia.** Yemen's Houthi rebels attacked Saudi oil sites three times in five days. They claimed a strike on the Rabigh refinery on the Red Sea coast; Saudi forces said they intercepted a missile. On Wednesday the large Ras Tanura refinery was hit, and oil loadings at the nearby terminal were reported suspended. On Thursday a strike on the Jazan refinery halted some operations and killed a worker.

**Global and U.S. oil split.** Brent, the international benchmark, rose about 3% to $95.44 on its expiring January contract, its first weekly gain this month. U.S. crude barely moved, down about 0.4% to $83.08. U.S. natural gas fell about 7% on the January contract. The price gap between Brent for January and for February nearly doubled, a sign the market is worried about oil available right now more than oil next year.

**Refiners lagged.** The profit margin on turning crude into diesel narrowed again, by about $7 a barrel. Marathon Petroleum and Valero fell for the week even as the rest of the sector rose.

**The rest bounced.** Companies that had fallen hardest in the prior week's market sell-off rose most: SLB +8%, Baker Hughes +6%, Kinder Morgan +6%. The fund held its 200-day average, a long-term trend line, every day.

**What's next.** OPEC+, the group of oil-producing countries led by Saudi Arabia and Russia, meets Sunday, November 29, to set 2027 production limits. Some members want higher limits, while forecasters warn of too much oil next year if Gulf exports return to normal.

**The Bottom Line:** A real supply threat, a muted price response. Sunday's OPEC+ decision is the next big test.
