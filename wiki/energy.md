## THE WEEKLY NARRATIVE — What's Actually Moving XLE

### This Week's Story: Earnings Season Opens With a Crack-Spread Bang

Last week the energy story was a war premium compounding: WTI +8.3%, Brent touching $100, and every sub-sector catching the bid. This week the story flipped to **earnings — and oil's fade**. WTI settled at **$84.67**, down roughly **$4.64 a barrel (-5.2%)** — its first weekly decline since the Iran escalation began. Brent was the bigger loser, dropping nearly **8%** as front-month contracts reflected both US-Iran negotiation headlines and a general risk-off tone in commodities.

**The earnings season opened with a refining supernova.** **Valero (VLO)** reported Q2 on Thursday and delivered an absolute blowout: **EPS of $12.54 versus $10.11 consensus**, a beat of $2.43, on revenue of $44.48B (+48.8% YoY). The stock responded by printing a fresh 52-week high at $312.90. Crack spreads remain at generational highs, and Valero's capture rate — the spread between crude input and refined product output — is the best in the sector. The market no longer cares that VLO trades above its mean analyst target; when you earn $12.54 in a quarter, target prices are revised, not ceilings.

**Chevron (CVX)** also beat on Friday morning: **$6.06 EPS versus $5.55 consensus**, with revenue surging 57% year-over-year to $67.20B. The market loved the Tengiz expansion update and the Permian production growth. CVX closed at $196.83, up 2.35% on the week, and the post-earnings gap held — a sign of institutional accumulation.

**ExxonMobil (XOM)** was the outlier. The company reported **$3.52 EPS, missing the $3.56 consensus by $0.04**, despite a revenue beat ($114.53B vs $109.94B est). The miss was minor, but in a market that had priced perfection into the largest energy name, the stock slid modestly. The Permian production growth was solid, but capital return guidance was unchanged — no dividend hike, no buyback acceleration. The market wanted more.

**SLB gave back its post-earnings pop**, falling from $52.42 to $49.59 (-5.4%) as crude's fade reminded investors that services stocks are a derivative of oil price direction, not just earnings beats. International revenue growth is real, but if Brent breaks below $85, E&P capex guidance gets cut — and SLB's thesis weakens.

**The macro overlay offered no new bullish catalysts.** OPEC+ already approved +188k bpd for August; that is priced in. Russia remains ~600k bpd below quota due to drone attacks. The SPR at 360M barrels is still a 40-year low. And the Abqaiq attack on Saudi Aramco's processing complex — while contained — served as a reminder that infrastructure attacks are now part of the playbook. Hormuz traffic actually *increased* slightly as US-Iran negotiations replaced missile strikes, but the risk premium has not fully evaporated.

**The Bottom Line:** The market is no longer asking "how high?" — it is asking "how fast does this fade?" The answer this week was: faster than the bulls hoped. WTI down 5.2%, Brent down 8%, and XLE flat despite massive earnings beats from two of its top three holdings. What the war giveth in two weeks, peace talks can taketh away in a week. RSI-14 at 68 is a healthier reading than 86, but it is not an all-clear. The sector is now a two-horse race: refining (crack spreads) and E&P (oil price). Services is lagging again.

### ⚠️ The Wild Card
The U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve is at **360M barrels — the lowest since 1983**. If the administration announces a refill program (buying oil at $70–$75 to rebuild the SPR), that creates a massive price floor. If they do not refill and a supply shock hits, there is no buffer. The SPR policy is the most underpriced factor in the energy market right now.
