**Sector Bifurcation:** Utilities continues its **generational split** between two completely different businesses:

**The Traditional Regulated Utilities (The Bond Proxies):**
- NEE, SO, DUK, D, AEP, SRE, EXC, XEL, PPL, ED, WEC, ES, DTE
- These are the classic utilities — regulated, stable, dividend-paying, boring
- They earn a regulated return on rate base. Growth = population + GDP + rate cases.
- They remain hostage to the 10Y yield. When rates rise, these stocks face headwinds.
- This week: most were flat to slightly higher. NEE +0.49%, DUK +2.50%, AEP +1.34%.

**The Nuclear / Data Center Utilities (The Growth Stories):**
- CEG (Constellation Energy) — nuclear fleet, data center power deals, SMR development
- VST (Vistra) — nuclear + gas fleet, Texas power market, data center demand
- These are NOT bond proxies. They are growth stocks masquerading as utilities.
- Their earnings are driven by power prices, nuclear relicensing, and data center contracts.
- They trade like tech stocks — high volatility, narrative-driven, momentum-sensitive.
- This week: CEG +9.46%, VST +10.76%. The narrative is back.

**The CEG Story:** Constellation Energy at $274.35, +9.46% this week, **33% below its 52W high** ($412.70). CEG is the largest nuclear fleet operator in the U.S. — 21 reactors across the country. The bull case: (1) nuclear power is the only zero-carbon baseload power source, (2) data centers need 24/7 power and are signing long-term contracts with nuclear operators, (3) the Department of Energy is supporting SMR (small modular reactor) development, (4) power prices in the PJM market are rising. The bear case: (1) CEG ran from $100 to $412 in 12 months — a 4x move that priced in perfection, (2) the correction to $228 was a 45% drawdown that shook out weak holders, (3) nuclear relicensing is expensive and uncertain, (4) at 23.8x P/E and 0.62% yield, this is not a utility — it is a growth stock. This week's +9.46% bounce suggests the narrative is reasserting.

**The VST Story:** Vistra at $163.38, +10.76% this week, **26% below its 52W high** ($219.82). Vistra is a power generation company — nuclear, gas, and renewable assets in Texas and the PJM market. The Texas power market (ERCOT) is volatile — prices can spike to $9,000/MWh during heat waves. Vistra benefits from price volatility. The company is also signing data center power deals. At 27.3x P/E and 0.56% yield, VST is not a utility — it is a leveraged bet on Texas power prices and data center demand. This week's surge suggests the market is pricing in a hot summer and strong power prices.

**Cecil Fundamental Read:** EIX at 8.7x P/E and 4.40% yield remains the cheapest utility in the sector. But Edison International has wildfire liability risk — California's inverse condemnation law makes utilities liable for wildfires caused by their equipment, regardless of negligence. The 2018 Camp Fire (PG&E) and the 2025 Eaton Fire (SCE) are cautionary tales. At 8.7x P/E, the market is pricing in significant wildfire risk. The 4.40% yield is a consolation prize for that risk. ES at 16.0x P/E and 4.22% yield is interesting — Eversource is a New England utility with stable regulation. EXC at 17.4x P/E and 3.53% yield is reasonable. DUK at 20.1x P/E and 3.36% yield is the gold standard of regulated utilities. But CEG at 23.8x P/E and 0.62% yield is not a utility — it is a nuclear growth stock. VST at 27.3x P/E and 0.56% yield is a leveraged bet on Texas power prices. I want utilities that pay dividends and keep the lights on. I do not want utilities that trade like meme stocks. But I cannot ignore a +10% week in VST.

### ⚠️ The Wild Card
The **weather** is the wild card for utilities. A hotter-than-expected summer drives electricity demand spikes — good for unregulated generators (VST, NRG) but mixed for regulated utilities (higher costs, potential rate case delays). A colder-than-expected winter drives natural gas demand spikes — good for gas utilities (SRE, DTE) but bad for customers (higher bills, political pressure). Extreme weather events (hurricanes, ice storms, wildfires) can cause billions in damage and trigger liability issues. The 2025 California wildfire season is ongoing. Any new fire linked to utility equipment could trigger another leg down for EIX and PCG. The weather is the one factor that no model can predict — and it can make or break a utility's quarter.
