**The META Story:** Meta at $595.19, -7.9% on the week, **25% below its 52W high** ($796.25) — the stock is now firmly in correction territory. The decline came without company-specific news; it was pure macro-driven multiple compression as the 10Y surged and growth-stock sentiment soured. The fundamental story from last week remains intact: the HSBC upgrade on the potential $10B Anthropic compute deal, and eMarketer's projection that Meta passes Alphabet in global net ad revenue in 2026 ($243.5B vs. $239.5B). But the market is no longer pricing fundamentals — it is pricing the cost of capital. At ~21.6x trailing P/E (down from ~23.5x), META is approaching value territory for a 25%+ grower. Q2 earnings land **Jul 29 AMC (confirmed)**. The bar is lower than it was two weeks ago, but the macro crosscurrents are nastier.

**The GOOGL Story:** Alphabet at $319.74, -7.8% on the week, **22% below its 52W high** ($408.61). The stock has now given back virtually all of its 2026 gains and is trading near its 200-day moving average (~$324). Like META, there was no fresh company-specific catalyst for the drop — it was rates, rates, and more rates. The stock is now 31% below its 52W high and below its 200D MA (~$200). TMUS had been the only green name in the top 10 last week; this week it joined the red parade.

**The CHTR Story:** Charter reported Q2 revenue of $13.5B (-1.7% YoY), with EPS of $10.76 beating the ~$10.00 estimate. ### This Week's Story: The Barbell Breaks on the Platform Side

For growth stocks, this is poison: the cost of capital rose, discount rates expanded, and multiples compressed. **2. The telecom earnings barbell effect.** VZ (Jul 22) and T (Jul 23) both reported solid Q2 beats and rallied hard (+8.3% and +12.6% respectively). VZ's $1.30 EPS beat on 348K broadband net adds and raised guidance confirmed the fiber/wireless strategy is working. T's $0.65 EPS beat on best-ever Q2 fiber additions proved the turnaround is real. But here's the math: T + VZ together are ~10% of XLC. META + GOOGL are ~38%. When the big weights fall 8% and the small weights rise 10%, the ETF still drops 4.8%. The barbell broke because the heavy end is too heavy.

**3. TMUS and CHTR disappointment.** T-Mobile reported Q2 EPS of $2.85, beating estimates, but the stock fell 6.5% because postpaid subscriber adds of ~250K were light — proof that even the share-taker is losing momentum. Together, TMUS + CHTR are another ~8% of XLC, and their declines amplified the platform weakness.

**The platforms ahead of the print.** META and GOOGL report Jul 28–29 into exactly this crosscurrent: their stocks are 22–25% off highs, P/Es have compressed 15–20%, and the market has already priced in significant disappointment. The question is whether the disappointment is fully priced. META's Q2 consensus expects ad revenue growth in the high-teens and capex commentary around the $125–145B guidance. GOOGL's Q2 expects Search growth >+10%, Cloud >+25%, and no new antitrust bombshells. With sentiment this negative, even an in-line quarter could trigger a relief rally. But a miss — on ad revenue, on cloud growth, or on capex discipline — and XLC visits $100.

**The Bottom Line:** The sector's technical picture has flipped from "cautiously bullish" to "confirmed breakdown." XLC below the 200D is a trend change. The only reason to own the ETF here is a bet that GOOGL and META both beat next week. That is a bet, not a position. The safer trade: own the telecoms (T, VZ) for income and yield-spread safety, and wait for the platform prints before touching META or GOOGL. EA at 52-week highs is the quiet outlier — the gaming cycle is working even as the ad cycle stumbles.
