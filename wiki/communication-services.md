## THE WEEKLY NARRATIVE — What's Actually Moving XLC

> *This section is rewritten weekly by the Saturday Research Crew. It captures the story beneath the headlines.*

### Week of November 9 – 13, 2026: Not Chips, and That Was Enough

XLC rose **3.7% to $108.69**, its second straight weekly gain, and beat the S&P 500's 2.6%. It ended the week just below its 50-day average.

**A rate scare that missed.** October consumer prices rose faster than expected, and futures moved to price a likely Fed rate hike in December. The 10-year Treasury yield reached 5.15%. High-dividend stocks such as Verizon and AT&T usually suffer when bond yields jump; both rose. Money leaving chip stocks after Nvidia's chief executive resigned found its way into companies with steady cash flow, and this fund has many of them.

**Charter's bond deal.** Charter offered to buy back up to $1.5 billion of bonds that mature decades from now, at prices well below face value. The stock rose almost 11% that day and 17% for the week, even as smaller cable companies fell.

**Games and movies.** Take-Two's quarterly bookings fell 18% and missed its own forecast, but the company said advance orders for Grand Theft Auto VI, which launches November 19, are far above plan. The stock rose 6%. Paramount Skydance, reporting for the first time since buying Warner Bros. Discovery, said it will stop publishing quarterly subscriber numbers and warned that weak Warner films could cut fourth-quarter profit. Its stock fell 6%.

**The giants.** Meta rose 5% and Alphabet about 4%, recovering some of their October losses. No company-specific news was found to explain either move.

**Small caps.** Magnite, an ad-tech firm, closed at a one-year high after a strong quarter driven by streaming TV and political ads. Ziff Davis, which owns CNET and IGN, said its traffic from Google search fell 37% in a year and declined to give a full-year forecast.

**What did not happen.** No final judgment was found in the Google ad-tech case, and no advertiser announced a budget cut.

**The Bottom Line:** The fund is being bought for what it is not. Disney's report on Tuesday and the GTA VI launch on Thursday are the first tests of what it is.
