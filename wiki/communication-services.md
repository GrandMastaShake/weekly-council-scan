**Sector Split:** XLC remains dominated by META (~22%) and GOOGL (~16% combined Class A + C) = ~38% of the fund. The rest splits across streaming (NFLX, DIS, WBD), telecom (T, VZ, TMUS), gaming (TTWO), and cable/broadband (CHTR, CMCSA).

**The META Story:** Meta at $616.77, **+6.70% on the week** — by far the sector's best performer and a new multi-month high. The catalyst was largely spillover relief from Judge Brinkema's September 2 ruling in the DOJ's ad-tech case against Google: the court rejected the government's push for a structural AdX breakup, imposing behavioral remedies instead [web:31][web:35]. That outcome removes the tail risk of a broader ad-tech industry restructuring that could have complicated Meta's own advertising business, and the market re-rated META sharply higher on reduced regulatory contagion risk. The stock is now well clear of its 50-day and closing in on 52-week highs, extending the post-settlement repair rally noted in prior updates.

**The GOOGL Story:** Alphabet fell 2.35% (GOOG -2.21%) even though Google technically won the headline antitrust remedies fight — Judge Leonie Brinkema declined to force a sale of the AdX exchange, the third time in recent years Big Tech has defeated a DOJ-sought breakup [web:28][web:29][web:74]. But the ruling still requires Google to make its ad tech interoperate with rivals' platforms, and the reasoning remains under seal for about 14 days, leaving uncertainty about the practical scope of the behavioral remedies [web:32][web:78]. The "sell the news" reaction reflects the market digesting that the compliance obligations, while short of a breakup, are real and open-ended, on top of a broader tech-multiple reset from the week's rate-driven selloff.

**The NFLX Story:** Netflix fell 4.25% to $78.25, giving back some of its recent gains amid the broader growth-stock de-rating tied to the hawkish jobs print and higher long-end yields, which weigh disproportionately on high-multiple names.

**The TTWO Story — the week's worst mover:** Take-Two dropped 8.79% as the GTA VI leak scandal escalated. The company has pursued federal court subpoenas against Microsoft and Discord, filed in the Southern District of New York, to identify the source(s) behind the "Cyberleek" persona spreading gameplay leaks, with compliance deadlines around September 4 [web:27][web:75][web:77]. While the leaks do not directly threaten the game's confirmed release, the sustained negative headline cycle around security and IP protection weighed heavily on sentiment this week.

**The DIS Story:** Disney fell 2.60% amid the same broad rate-driven de-rating affecting consumer-discretionary-adjacent media names, with no major company-specific catalyst this week.

**The Telecom Story:** The barbell's defensive half was mixed but essentially flat — VZ +0.08% and TMUS +0.08% held steady even as the 10-year pushed higher, while T slipped 1.35% and CHTR fell 1.06%. Given the hawkish jobs print and higher yields, telecom's near-flat performance (versus growth-side declines in GOOGL/NFLX/DIS) shows the dividend names are proving somewhat more resilient than in a typical rate-shock week, though the group is not rallying either.

**The WBD Story:** Warner Bros. Discovery slipped 1.81% to $28.25; the M&A/litigation framework from prior weeks (frozen settlement track, March 2027 trial, ticking fee from October 1) remains unchanged.

**Cecil Fundamental Read:** META's re-rating on reduced ad-tech regulatory risk is the week's genuine fundamental positive — a real de-risking event, not just a headline pop. GOOGL's pullback despite a favorable court outcome is a reminder that "no breakup" still means "must interoperate," a real compliance cost whose scope isn't yet quantified until the sealed opinion is released. TTWO's leak-driven selloff is sentiment, not fundamentals — GTA VI's release timeline is unaffected — but the stock deserves a discount for governance/security noise until the subpoena matter passes.

## THE WEEKLY NARRATIVE — What's Actually Moving XLC

> *This section is rewritten weekly by the Saturday Research Crew. It captures the story beneath the headlines.*

### This Week's Story: One Ruling, Two Opposite Stock Reactions — Plus a Hawkish Jobs Shock

**1. Brinkema's ad-tech ruling split META and GOOGL in opposite directions.** Judge Leonie Brinkema's September 2 decision rejecting the DOJ's push to force Google to divest its AdX ad exchange and DFP publisher ad server — instead ordering behavioral and interoperability remedies — was reported as a clear win for Google, the third Big Tech breakup defeat in recent memory [web:31][web:35][web:74]. Yet GOOGL fell 2.35% on the week while META rallied 6.70%. The market's read: Google avoided the worst-case structural outcome, but still faces real, open-ended compliance costs whose details remain sealed for roughly two weeks, while Meta simply benefits from reduced contagion risk to the broader digital-ad ecosystem without bearing any of the compliance burden itself.

**2. A blowout jobs report reset the rate conversation.** August nonfarm payrolls printed +162K against a roughly 53,000 consensus, resolving the "weak labor market" scare that had persisted since July's negative print [web:47], but the resulting hawkish repricing pushed the 10-year Treasury to 4.78-4.79% — above the 4.75% stress line for a second straight week. For a sector that includes both high-multiple growth platforms and dividend-sensitive telecoms, higher-for-longer rates are a two-sided headwind.

**3. Oil's renewed spike adds a fresh inflation wrinkle.** WTI crude jumped roughly 9.7% on the week to around $91/barrel as the US and Iran resumed military exchanges, reviving Middle East supply-disruption fears. Sustained oil strength complicates the disinflation narrative the Fed needs to justify future rate cuts, adding another reason the market priced September hawkishly.

**4. Take-Two's leak scandal escalated into a real stock-price problem.** The company's federal subpoenas against Microsoft and Discord over the ongoing GTA VI leaks from the "Cyberleek" persona, first reported in late August, drove an 8.79% decline — the sector's worst performer by a wide margin [web:27][web:75][web:77]. The leaks don't threaten the confirmed November release, but the sustained negative headline cycle around security is weighing on sentiment.

**5. The telecom sleeve held up reasonably well.** VZ and TMUS both closed essentially flat (+0.08% each) even as the growth side of the sector sold off on the rate shock — a modest sign of relative resilience in the dividend half of the barbell, though not the outright rally seen in prior weeks.

**The Bottom Line:** This was a week where sector-specific legal/security catalysts (the Google ruling, the GTA VI leaks) mattered more than the macro backdrop for individual stock moves, even though the macro backdrop (hot jobs, rising oil, sticky yields) set the overall risk tone. September's CPI and FOMC decisions are next up to determine whether the hawkish repricing that began this week persists.
