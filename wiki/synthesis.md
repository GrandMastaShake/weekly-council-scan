## 1. CECIL'S SYNTHESIS — Fundamentalist Lens

At a 4.975% ten-year, carry stopped being a preference and became the organizing principle. The week's most useful fundamental observation is not which sectors fell — nearly all of them did — but that **the sorting happened inside sectors, along the yield-versus-multiple line, in six places at once.**

Consumer staples gave the cleanest proof. The four cheapest dividend payers in the top ten all finished green or flat — **PM +4.67%, MDLZ +1.89%, KO +0.25%, MO +0.15%** — while the expensive defensives bled: **COST -1.20% at ~45.5x, CL -2.22% at ~34.2x**. That is a 300-to-600 basis point spread inside one sector in four sessions, decided entirely by what each name yields against a risk-free rate that keeps climbing.

**The dividend-yield spreads that actually matter this week are the negative ones, and they are at cycle extremes.** Utilities' 2.83% SEC yield sits **215 bps below** the 10Y — the worst income penalty of the entire 2026 rate breakout. Staples' ~2.6% sits **238 bps below**, the widest negative carry of the cycle. Real estate's ~3.19% sits **178 bps below**, also a cycle worst. **Not one dividend sector ETF in this book now clears the risk-free rate.** Only individual names do, and the list is short and instructive: **O at 5.48% (+50 bps over the 10Y), CCI at 5.62% (+65 bps), VZ at 5.6%, MO at 6.4%, UPS at 6.56%, EIX at 6.27%.** Cecil marks two of those as coupons rather than income — UPS's yield is the market pricing the payout's risk, not its generosity, and EIX's 6.27% is "a litigation coupon," with PG&E's $2B cut to its 2027 capex plan the reminder that California liability has become a solvency conversation, not a valuation one.

**The week's defining fundamental event was ORCL, and it was a warning about the denominator.** Q1 FY27 delivered revenue $19.35B (+30%), adjusted EPS $1.92 against $1.74 consensus (+10.3%), OCI +121% to $7.4B, RPO of **$664B (+$26B in a single quarter)**, 850MW delivered, 97.9% GPU utilization, four-year-old GPUs renewing at +20% premiums, and a raised FY27 to ≥$90B revenue. When a 10% EPS beat with a raised year gets sold the same morning, the discount rate is running the tape, and long-dated capex is what it is taxing.

**Value versus traps.** The restocked value shelf: **EOG at 11.5x** (cheapest quality barrel-owner), **MPC 13.7x / VLO 16.3x** on record diesel cracks, the money centers at **WFC 13.1x / BAC 14.4x / C 14.9x**, **PEP 17.9x at a 4.3% yield**, **NKE 17.5x at 4.5%**, **LOW 16.6x**, **EXC 15.9x at 3.89%**, **GOOGL at 17x**, **SPG at 14.5x**, **CUBI at 9.5x** against a $94 target.
