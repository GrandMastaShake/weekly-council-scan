**Narrative:** This week delivered the falsification the Council has been waiting five weeks for — and it resolved in the opposite direction from July's shock. The broken **NFP < 150K flag**, alive since July's stunning -23K print, is now **closed**: August payrolls came in at +162,000, nearly triple the ~53-56K consensus, with unemployment holding at 4.1% and participation actually improving. That is an unambiguous "the freeze was noise" verdict on the headline number.

**Act I — The Soft Underbelly (Monday-Tuesday).** A quiet Labor-Day-adjacent Monday gave way to Tuesday's ISM Manufacturing miss: 54.6 against a 55.2 consensus, down from July's near-four-year high of 55.6. Manufacturing employment slowed to 51.2 from 52.8. The flash PMI's manufacturing cooling — first flagged three weeks ago — is now ISM-confirmed.

**Act II — The Labor Data Split (Wednesday-Thursday).** ADP delivered the week's first genuine warning: private payrolls added just 38,000 jobs, missing the ~47-48K consensus and marking the slowest pace since January, with manufacturing (-17K) and professional services (-16K) both shedding jobs. The Fed's own Beige Book corroborated it — employment rose "very slightly," with retail and hospitality demand falling. Then Thursday flipped the script: ISM Services beat decisively at 55.4 (from 54.1), even as its own employment sub-index contracted for a second straight month (47.8) and prices paid hit a four-year high (72.6). Fed Governor Waller used the same day to signal he's "inclined to support" holding rates steady — a dovish counterweight from inside the building.

**Act III — The Verdict (Friday).** August nonfarm payrolls smashed every estimate: +162,000 against a consensus near 53-56,000, unemployment unchanged at 4.1%, and labor-force participation actually improving to 61.6%. This is the single loudest print of the last two months, and it resolved hawkish: CME September hike odds jumped from roughly 49% to over 60%, and the 10-year Treasury closed at 4.78-4.79%, its highest level in years. Equities shrugged — SPY finished essentially flat on the week — but semiconductors and other high-beta growth actually **rallied** on the print (see wiki/semiconductors.md), a genuine divergence from the "hawkish data = sell growth" script that governed late August.

**The Bottom Line:** The labor-market crack that defined the Council's macro thesis for five weeks did not survive its own falsification test. NFP is resolved bullish-for-growth/hawkish-for-the-Fed. ISM Manufacturing is the new soft spot. ADP's miss and the Beige Book's "very slightly" language say the labor market is genuinely two-speed, even if the headline aggregate just posted its best print since spring. The next and more consequential test is CPI on September 11, four days before the FOMC decides.

**🚨 Regime flags carried, resolved, or raised:** **NFP < 150K is RESOLVED / no longer broken** (August +162K decisively breaks the negative streak — the week's single biggest regime change). DXY < 100 remains **broken** (sixth straight week, though it eased back to 99.13-99.15 from 99.68 the prior week — the hawkish dollar-rebuild thesis stalled). **Retail Sales < -0.1% stays broken** on the last confirmed print (July -0.6%; August data due mid-September). Net: one flag resolved, one flag intensified — the Council Review posture stays in force, but the character of the risk has shifted from "labor crack" toward "rates crack."
