**Narrative:** This week delivered the Fed's self-declared "final determinant" before its most consequential meeting of the year, and the data gave it no clean answer. August core CPI rose 0.3% month-over-month — a tenth hotter than consensus, and the first monthly core print to run hot since the disinflation trend took hold this summer — even as the year-over-year core rate matched expectations at 2.4%, the lowest annual reading since March 2021. That is a genuinely split verdict: the Fed's preferred trend measure kept improving while the freshest monthly data point, the one markets trade off in real time, broke the wrong way.

It did not arrive alone. Thursday's PPI report front-ran it with an outsized energy shock — diesel fuel prices jumped 24.1% for the month, a direct pass-through from the oil market's second front (WTI's first-ever $100+ settle this week, see wiki/energy.md) — pulling headline producer prices up 0.4% and driving the 10-year Treasury more than 10 basis points higher in a single session, its sharpest daily move since the August jobs report. By Friday's CPI release, the 10-year had already priced in most of the hawkish case; the print's marginal miss simply pushed it the rest of the way to a fresh 52-week high of 4.975%, now within 2.5 basis points of the round-number 5.00% level the Council has flagged as a crisis threshold. CME FedWatch odds of a September 16 rate hike vaulted from roughly 60% a week ago to somewhere in the 85-90% range — a hike is now close to a foregone conclusion.

The week's other genuine shock came from the consumer, not the Fed: the University of Michigan's preliminary September sentiment reading collapsed to 47.8, the second-lowest level on record, as rising fuel prices and trade-tension anxiety pushed year-ahead inflation expectations to 4.6%, the highest since June. That the S&P still finished the week essentially flat — absorbing a hot inflation surprise, a bond-market shock, and a consumer-sentiment collapse in the same five sessions — is itself the most important fact of the week. Either positioning had already priced in enough bad news that a mixed resolution counted as relief, or risk assets are choosing to look through the entire macro picture until the FOMC actually speaks. Next week resolves which.

**🚨 Regime flags carried, resolved, or raised:** DXY < 100 **stays broken** (7th consecutive week, essentially flat at ~99.12 — the dollar still has not caught up to the hawkish rate repricing). The **10Y > 4.75% stress line is broken for a THIRD consecutive week and is now within 2.5bp of the 5.00% crisis line** (4.975% close) — the single most important number on the board heading into the FOMC. **CPI MoM > 0.3% is newly broken** — the first monthly core print to hit this threshold since the disinflation trend began, even as the YoY trend kept improving. **Retail Sales < -0.1% stays broken** on stale data (July -0.6%), with the real test landing next Wednesday. **WTI > $100 is newly broken** (first sustained $100+ settle of the conflict regime, cross-referencing wiki/energy.md) — the oil shock that fed this week's PPI surprise is not resolved, it is escalating.
