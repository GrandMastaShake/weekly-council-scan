**Narrative:** The thesis on this page has been "a Fed that will hike again, a labor market that has stopped hiring, and an oil shock that is not yet in the core," and last week it bent toward "growth scare with sticky inflation." This week's data partly confirmed that turn and partly argued with it.

Housing confirmed it. Starts fell to 1.221M against 1.30M, single-family permits hit their lowest since March 2023, and builder sentiment fell to 29. With mortgage rates above 7%, housing is the one sector where the Fed's hikes are clearly working. Existing home sales beat at 3.99M, but that is closings on contracts signed in the summer.

Everything else contradicted it. Claims printed 194K, a sixth week under 200K. The flash PMI composite was 57.6, with manufacturing at a 53-month high and output prices rising at the fastest pace since August 2022. UMich beat every estimate at 48.0, with 5-year inflation expectations at 3.5%. China grew 4.5%. That is not an economy in a growth scare; it is a hot, inflationary economy with a broken housing market.

The bond market traded the scare anyway. The 10-year fell 19bp on Thursday, the day SOX fell 6.7% after Lam's supply comment and Meta's reversal, and closed the week at 4.988%, the first weekly close under 5.00% in five weeks. October hike odds drifted to ~10% with the Fed in blackout. As in the last two weeks, the rally came from equity stress, not from the data.

Then Friday the December WTI contract settled at $103.27 on Hormuz headlines, and the PMI said firms are passing on costs. So the regime is still **"sticky inflation, a December hike, and a financial-conditions growth scare."** The difference this week is that the 10-year flag cleared while the oil flag is one close from firing. Next week tests both: September PCE and Q3 GDP on Thursday, the FOMC on Wednesday.
