**Sticky inflation: confirmed.** October CPI ran +0.4% against +0.3%, with core +0.3% against +0.2%. It is the third straight hot core print, and shelter, not energy, did the work: oil fell in October. PPI's headline was softer than feared (+0.3% vs +0.4%), but core was in line at +0.3%, the annual rate hit 6.5%, the highest since 2022, and September was revised up. Last week's argument was wages hot, unit labor costs cool. CPI settled it on the hot side.

**A December hike: confirmed and priced.** FedWatch went from ~72–79% to **~88–89%**, with a 50bp tail of 9–14%. Waller said the Fed ought to hike. The front end led: the 5-year rose 33bp, more than the 10-year (+29.5bp) or the 30-year (+22bp). That is a bear flattener, the market pricing policy, not term premium. **The 10-year closed over 5.00% on Thursday and at 5.154% on Friday**, and the dollar made a 52-week high.

**The growth scare: reversed, for now.** VIX fell from 25.6 to 19.3 and cleared its flag on Monday. SPY rose 2.6% and ended a four-week losing streak. The bounce came from CoreWeave's raised forecast and AMAT's guide, which answered last week's data-center funding and permitting fear with numbers. The one equity shock, NVIDIA's CEO resignation, stayed inside one stock: nine of SMH's top ten rose.

**The one soft spot is housing:** existing home sales fell 5.5% to 3.75M with mortgage rates over 7%, and claims at 200K ended the run of beats. Neither is a labor or consumer break.

**So the regime has moved one notch hawkish.** The data no longer disagree with the hike; the market has stopped fighting it. **The risk is no longer a growth scare that cuts the Fed's legs out. It is a Fed that hikes into a 10-year already over 5%.**
