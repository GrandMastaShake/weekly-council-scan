**Narrative:** The week answered one question and raised another. The question it answered: is the labor market cracking? Thursday's jobless claims print said **no — emphatically**. Initial claims plunged to 187,000, the lowest reading since 1969, obliterating the 210K consensus and sending the 4-week moving average down to 207.5K. Continuing claims fell to 1.796 million. The labor market that looked fragile in June (NFP +57K) suddenly looked bulletproof in July. The bond market voted immediately — Treasury yields rose, the 2-year firmed above 4.30%, and any residual pricing for a September rate cut evaporated.

The question the week raised: what happens when strong labor data collides with an oil shock? Friday's session was dominated by renewed Middle East tensions — attacks on Saudi oil tankers, escalating U.S.-Iran rhetoric — that sent Brent crude briefly above $100 per barrel for the first time since May. The S&P 500 fell 0.6% Friday, the Nasdaq dropped more, and the semiconductor complex sold off 3% as growth stocks recoiled from both higher oil and higher real rates. Cross-asset: DXY held firm near 101.5, the 10-year finished at 4.69% (up from ~4.58% a week ago), and VIX climbed toward 20.

The equity tape's message was clear: good news is bad news again. Strong labor data means the Fed has no cover to cut. Higher oil means inflation may re-accelerate. The disinflation victory of June (CPI −0.4%, core flat) is now being stress-tested by energy prices and a Fed that remains on hold. The Council's regime call shifts one notch: from *stagflation-lite watch* to **stagflation-lite confirmed** — growth is sluggish (PMI composite 51.8, Chicago Fed near zero), inflation is falling on the core but re-energized by oil, and the labor market is too strong to justify cuts. The Fed's policy box is locked.
