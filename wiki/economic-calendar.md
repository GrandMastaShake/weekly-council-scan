**Narrative:** This week was a three-act play with a plot twist in every act. **Act I — The Setup:** Monday's durable goods miss (+0.3% vs +1.5%) and Tuesday's soft consumer confidence (90.8 vs 92.0) primed the market for bad news. The tape held its breath. **Act II — The Shock:** Wednesday's FOMC decision was supposed to be the non-event of the summer — a hold at 3.50–3.75% was ~70% priced. Instead, the Fed delivered a **9-3 vote** with Hammack, Kashkari, and Logan all dissenting for a +25bp hike. Warsh's presser was hawkish to the point of defiant: "will deliver price stability," inflation "elevated." The bond market panicked — the 10-year surged to **4.74%**, a fresh multi-year high, and SPY sold off 1.5% intraday before clawing back half the losses. The message was unmistakable: the Fed is divided, and the hawks are not silent.

**Act III — The Relief:** Thursday's double print — GDP Q2 advance at **1.5% SAAR** (miss vs ~2.0%) and core PCE at **+0.1% MoM** (cool vs +0.2% consensus) — arrived in the same 8:30 AM minute. The GDP miss confirmed the slowdown narrative; the PCE cool confirmed the disinflation narrative. Together they said: the Fed is on hold not because it wants to be, but because the data is doing its work. SPY rallied 1.7%. The 10-year yield dipped, then firmed as the market remembered three Fed members wanted to hike yesterday. Friday's ECI (3.4% YoY, steady) and Michigan sentiment beat (55.2) added no new drama. SPY closed the week at **$747.03**, up 1.1% from Monday's open, and the S&P 500 notched a two-day rally into month-end — even as July still logged its first monthly decline since 2014.

The cross-asset picture shifted materially. **DXY broke below 100** for the first time in weeks, closing at **99.80** on Friday. That is the most important macro relief of the quarter — a weaker dollar lifts emerging markets, commodities, and multinational earnings. The 10-year at 4.74% is the counterweight — rates at multi-year highs cap valuation expansion. VIX collapsed from a post-FOMC spike of 20.66 to **15.99**, signaling the options market is no longer pricing extreme event risk. The Council's regime call holds: **stagflation-lite confirmed**, but the DXY break below 100 adds a new variable — dollar weakness may offset some of the rate pressure.

**🚨 Two regime flags are now raised:** DXY < 100 (broken Friday) and NFP < 150K (June's +57K remains below threshold). This triggers a **Council Review before Monday's session.**
