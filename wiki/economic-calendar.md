**Narrative:** The thesis on this page is "sticky inflation, a December hike, and a financial-conditions growth scare." This week's data confirmed all three, and the market spent Friday pricing only the third.

The hot-economy half held. GDP beat at 3.4%, claims were 194K, a seventh week under 200K, and durable goods beat. Conference Board confidence jumped to 91.7 against 81.0, which reverses the consumer-cracking worry this page carried from September. New home sales beat as well, though mortgages still pay 7.24–7.37%.

The sticky-inflation half held too, with one softer detail. Core PCE met consensus at +0.3% and 3.1%, so the under-call on inflation did not repeat, but it also did not cool. The oil flag fired Monday and December WTI ended at $104.12. Two details lean the other way: final sales were only 1.7% and real spending was flat in September, so the 3.4% was softer underneath than the headline.

The hike half got louder at the Fed. A 10-2 vote with a governor dissenting is unusual, and Musalem said "necessary, and soon." The market took the other side. After Intel and Amazon, December odds fell from ~83% to ~73–76%, and priced hikes through June 2027 fell from about 2.8 to 2.0. The 2-year fell 13bp. As in each of the last four weeks, the bond rally came from equity stress, not from a print.

So the regime is unchanged and the disagreement is wider: **the Fed and the data say hike; the tape says the AI selloff will do the work.** Payrolls and the October CPI (Nov 10) decide it.
