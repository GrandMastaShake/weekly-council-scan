**Narrative:** Last week's question was whether the 10-year would break 5.00% before the consumer broke. This week answered both parts, and the answers point the same hawkish way. The consumer did not break. August retail sales rose 1.2% against roughly 0.8-0.9% expected, with the control group up 1.4%, its strongest gain since September 2024. July's -0.6% now reads as a one-month air pocket rather than the start of a crack. The UMich collapse to 47.8 last week measured how consumers *feel*. Retail sales measured what they *did*, and they spent.

That made the Fed's decision easy and its message clear. The FOMC hiked 25bp to 3.75–4.00%, unanimously, its first increase since 2023. The dot plot then signaled this is a cycle rather than an insurance hike: 16 of 19 officials see at least one more move this year, and the SEP marked year-end PCE inflation up to 3.7%. Chair Warsh gave the market no dovish opening. The 10-year closed at 5.006% that afternoon, the first close above the Council's 5.00% crisis line and the highest yield since July 2007, and the 2-year hit a two-year high. Thursday's jobless claims (196K) and a Philly Fed beat with rising prices paid reinforced the point. By Friday, CME odds of an October hike were near 58%, up from 42% a week earlier. The Bank of Japan's hike to 1.25% on Friday added global pressure on long rates.

The data *contradicted* the "one-and-done" thesis that the market held into Wednesday and *confirmed* the no-landing-with-a-hawkish-Fed regime this page has carried since the August jobs report. The soft spots are real but secondary: Empire (7.6 vs 14.0), industrial production (flat), NAHB (32), permits (-2.7%) and the LEI (-0.1%) all show the rate-sensitive, goods-producing side cooling. In a hiking cycle that is the intended effect, not a reason to stop. What kept equities nearly flat (SPY -0.34%) was oil falling back to $95 and a chip-led rally Thursday. The bond market did not improve. The 10Y ended the week 0.2bp below 5.00%, pinned there.
