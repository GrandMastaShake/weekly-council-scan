## THE WEEKLY NARRATIVE — What's Actually Moving XLK

> *This section is rewritten weekly by the Saturday Research Crew. It captures the story beneath the headlines.*

### This Week's Story: The Great Divergence — MSFT's Earnings Detonation vs. the Semiconductor Rout

Last week this page warned that tech had entered a tense consolidation and that the heaviest earnings week of the season was upon us. The consolidation broke — but not in the direction most expected. XLK finished the week down a mere -0.3%, but the internal dispersion was the most extreme of 2026: MSFT surged +21.75% from Monday's open to Friday's close, while AMD fell -8.77%, MU plunged -10.63%, and AAPL crashed -7.24%. The S&P 500 finished the week roughly flat. The Nasdaq fell -0.6%. Tech is no longer a monolith — it is two separate trades, and this week the chasm widened.

**Catalyst 1 — Microsoft's Historic Beat (Wed–Thu):** Microsoft reported fiscal Q4 2026 after the close on Wednesday and delivered one of the most impressive quarters in its history. Revenue of $90.0 billion beat the ~$87.7 billion consensus by 2.6%. Adjusted EPS of $4.74 (excluding a $3.2B Anthropic investment gain) beat the ~$4.24 estimate by 11.8%. But the headline numbers were not the story — Azure and other cloud services revenue grew **43% YoY**, accelerating from 40% in the prior quarter. Microsoft Cloud revenue hit $59.3 billion (+27%). Commercial remaining performance obligation surged 84% sequentially to a record **$678 billion**. CEO Satya Nadella announced that Azure revenue surpassed $100 billion annually for the first time. The stock, which had closed Wednesday at $390.54, gapped up +15.5% Thursday to $451.10 and closed Friday at $464.72. The +21.75% weekly gain is one of the largest in MSFT's history as a mega-cap. Management guided FY27 capital expenditure to **$255–260 billion**, nearly double FY26's ~$190B. The market's verdict: the AI supercycle is not peaking; it is entering its second inning.

**Catalyst 2 — Apple's Guidance Miss (Thu–Fri):** Apple reported fiscal Q3 2026 after Thursday's close and on the surface delivered a clean beat: EPS of $2.02 vs $1.89 expected (+6.9%), revenue of $109.4B vs $109.0B expected. iPhone revenue of $54.3B (+22% YoY) and Services revenue of $30.7B (+12% YoY) were both solid. But the guidance was the killer: the September quarter revenue outlook of roughly $113 billion missed analyst estimates of ~$114.9 billion. For a stock that had been priced for perfection — trading above its mean analyst target just one week ago — a guidance miss was enough to trigger a -7.24% crash on Friday to $308.91. The stock gave back its entire post-July 17 rally in one session. The read-through for the sector: even the most defensive mega-cap tech name is not immune to the "sell the news" dynamic that punished Intel last week.

**Catalyst 3 — Meta's Capex Consumes Cash Flow (Wed):** Meta reported Q2 2026 on Wednesday evening alongside Microsoft and delivered a mixed bag. Revenue of $60.8B beat consensus (~$60.2B), with advertising revenue surging 27% YoY to $59.4B. But diluted EPS of $6.18 missed the ~$7.22 consensus by 14%, dragged down by a $2.4B legal charge and $1.18B in severance. The number that moved the stock: free cash flow collapsed **91% YoY** to just $784 million as quarterly capex of $31.1B consumed roughly 98% of operating cash flow. Meta narrowed its 2026 capex guidance to **$130–145B** (floor up $5B). The stock fell roughly 8–10% in after-hours trading Wednesday. The read-through: the market is no longer grading AI capex — it is grading the revenue attached to it. Meta's ad engine is strong, but the cash-flow math is deteriorating.

**Catalyst 4 — Amazon's AWS Acceleration (Thu):** Amazon reported Q2 2026 on Thursday evening and delivered a strong print. Net sales increased 20% YoY to $200.6B. Operating income surged 43% to $27.5B. AWS segment sales increased **37% YoY to $42.2 billion** — its fastest growth in 18 quarters, representing a $169 billion annualized revenue run rate. The stock reaction was positive but measured relative to MSFT's explosion. The read-through for the cloud AI trade: AWS growth is accelerating, not decelerating, and the three-hyperscaler narrative (MSFT Azure +43%, AMZN AWS +37%, GOOGL Cloud likely strong) suggests the infrastructure demand is real and broad-based.

**Catalyst 5 — The Semiconductor Selloff (Mon–Fri):** While software/cloud names surged on earnings, the semiconductor complex was routed. AMD fell -8.77% ahead of its Aug 4 earnings report. MU plunged -10.63% as profit-taking accelerated and concerns about memory cycle peak intensified — despite Micron's HBM capacity being sold out through 2027. AMAT fell -5.33% and LRCX fell -3.99% even though LRCX beat EPS estimates ($1.82 vs $1.68 expected). The market's verdict: the equipment and memory names have run too far, too fast, and the "AI capex bubble" narrative is now being applied to the supply chain rather than the end customers. The software-chip correlation inversion that began three weeks ago reached its most extreme point this week.

**Catalyst 6 — The Fed's Three Dissenters (Wed):** The FOMC held rates at 3.50–3.75% on Wednesday, as expected. But the vote was 9–3, with Beth Hammack, Neel Kashkari, and Lorie K. Logan all dissenting in favor of a 25bp hike. This is the first dissent of 2026 and a significant signal under new Chair Kevin Warsh. The market read it as a hawkish warning: the Fed may not cut in 2026, and a hike in September, October, or December is now a live possibility. Bank of America has projected three 25bp hikes in September, October, and December that would bring the fed funds rate to 4.50%. For a sector trading at 33x trailing earnings, a hiking cycle is the most dangerous macro scenario.

**The Bottom Line:** The AI capex thesis survived its biggest test of 2026. Microsoft's $255B FY27 guide, Azure's 43% growth, and AWS's 37% acceleration proved that demand is real and accelerating. But the market's tolerance for multiple expansion is zero until earnings prove the denominator — and even then, only the names that can meter the spend are being rewarded. The semiconductor supply chain is being questioned on cycle duration. The Fed's three dissenters added a new macro risk. After this week, the burden of proof has shifted: tech must now earn the multiple back, one report at a time — and the market is discriminating ruthlessly between the metered spenders and the commodity suppliers.
