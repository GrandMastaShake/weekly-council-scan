## THE WEEKLY NARRATIVE — What's Actually Moving XLK

> *This section is rewritten weekly by the Saturday Research Crew. It captures the story beneath the headlines.*

### This Week's Story: The Relief Rally — Yields Fall, Tech Flies

Last week this page warned that tech had entered a tense consolidation after the most violent earnings week of the season. The consolidation broke — to the upside. XLK surged +7.2%, its best weekly gain of 2026. The Nasdaq ripped +6.8%. The S&P 500 added +4.2%. Nearly every top holding finished green, and the semiconductor complex that was routed last week found aggressive buyers. The internal dispersion flipped from negative to positive — breadth expanded, and the rally had participation across software, semis, and equipment.

**Catalyst 1 — Treasury Yield Collapse (Mon–Fri):** The 10-year Treasury yield fell from 4.745% last Friday to 4.66% this Friday — an 8 basis point drop that may sound small but was transformative for a sector trading at 36x trailing earnings. The move was driven by two factors: first, WTI crude collapsed from ~$85 to ~$78, easing inflation fears and removing the "Fed must hike" narrative that had been building since the three dissenters emerged on July 29. Second, economic data showed signs of cooling — not crashing, but cooling enough to suggest the Fed's 3.50–3.75% rate is already restrictive. For XLK, every basis point lower in the 10-year is multiple expansion.

**Catalyst 2 — NVDA's Pre-Earnings Momentum (Mon–Fri):** NVIDIA surged +11.56% this week, rising from $200.75 to $223.96, adding roughly +1.5% to XLK's NAV alone. The stock is now within 5% of its 52-week high of $236.54. The catalyst was a combination of factors: Intel's continued foundry progress (which benefits the entire semiconductor ecosystem), a broader risk-on rotation into growth names, and the traditional "pre-earnings drift" as investors position ahead of NVDA's Aug. 26 report. The Street is looking for ~$2.08 EPS with data center revenue growth re-acceleration and potential Rubin architecture updates. NVDA at ~17x forward earnings with +35% implied upside to the $303 mean target is pricing in a beat — but not a blowout.

**Catalyst 3 — AMD's Earnings Digestion (Tue):** AMD reported Q2 FY2026 on Tuesday, Aug. 4, delivering results that were largely in-line with expectations. Revenue of ~$11.2B met the guidance range. Data Center revenue showed double-digit sequential growth. The MI400 ramp timeline was re-affirmed. The stock, which had fallen -8.77% last week into the report, rallied modestly +1.51% on the week — a "relief" move suggesting the bar had been lowered enough that in-line results were enough to stabilize the name. The read-through for the sector: the semiconductor cycle is not peaking, but the "beat and raise" era may be transitioning to a "meet and guide" era.

**Catalyst 4 — Intel's Foundry Firesale Reversal (Mon–Fri):** Intel exploded +12.69% this week, rising from $90.20 to $101.65. The stock has now nearly doubled from its 2025 lows. The catalyst was a combination of continued optimism around the Apple foundry partnership (first reported in June, but with fresh commentary this week suggesting progress on the 18A node) and a broader rotation into "value" semiconductor names that had been left behind. INTC's forward P/E of ~49x is expensive, but the narrative has shifted from "Intel is broken" to "Intel is a call option on U.S. semiconductor manufacturing." The stock still trades below its mean target of $115.17, leaving room if foundry milestones are hit.

**Catalyst 5 — SOUN's Earnings Detonation (Thu–Fri):** SoundHound AI reported Q2 2026 on Wednesday, Aug. 6, and the stock surged +30.8% on the week. While a sub-$5B name, SOUN's move is notable because it reflects the appetite for AI-adjacent small-caps when the large-cap trade gets crowded. The company has voice-AI partnerships with Stellantis and Hyundai, and the earnings report likely showed revenue acceleration or margin improvement. The read-through for the sector: AI demand is real at every market cap, and the "picks and shovels" trade is extending down the capitalization spectrum.

**Catalyst 6 — Crude Oil Collapse (Mon–Fri):** WTI crude fell from ~$84.67 to ~$78.18 — a -7.7% weekly drop that was as important as the Treasury rally for tech multiples. The driver was continued easing of Iran-Hormuz tensions and signs of demand softening in China. For the Fed, lower oil means lower headline inflation, which means less pressure to hike. For XLK, lower oil is a direct tailwind: it reduces the "stagflation" risk that had been the bear case since July, and it gives the Fed room to hold rates steady through year-end.

**The Bottom Line:** The tech sector just had its best week of 2026. The 10-year yield's retreat from 4.75% to 4.66% was the primary driver, but the rally was broad enough — semis, software, equipment, and even small-caps participated — to suggest something more than a rates trade. The post-earnings digestion from MSFT, AAPL, META, and AMZN resolved positively. NVDA's pre-earnings drift added momentum. INTC's foundry narrative gained steam. The burden of proof has shifted back to the bears: with the 50D MA recaptured, RSI at neutral, and yields falling, tech has the wind at its back until proven otherwise. The risk is that this was a low-volume, rates-driven rally that lacks institutional conviction — Friday's 6.0M volume vs 12.1M average is the yellow flag.
