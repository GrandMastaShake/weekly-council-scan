# The Council's debate logs -- week of 2026-08-24

Three members with three different jobs picked five names each, working apart and without seeing one another's picks.

## Ophelia -- sector rotation

Her four sectors this week:

- **Healthcare**: XLV made its first weekly close above the old $169.71 ceiling, hitting an all-time high of $176.60 on the heaviest volume of 2026, and 13 of the 14 names we track rose. It moves almost independently of SPY (correlation -0.10) and trades on its own news: the intismeran Phase 3 win, a run of FDA approvals, and GILD's HIV-drug decision due Aug 27. That matters more to it than this week's PCE, NVDA and Warsh events. I'm wrong if a strong NVDA report plus a patient Warsh pull money back into tech and XLV falls below $169.71 (then the $167.09 20-day average), or if drug-pricing or tariff headlines hit pharma.
- **Energy**: The oil premium now comes from a real supply cut, not headlines: Hormuz transits fell to 5 on Saturday and 0 on Sunday, the White House ruled out talks with Iran, and the weekly EIA stock build shrank to 4.4M barrels. XLE broke out to a 52-week high and moves against SPY (correlation -0.38), so it is the book's hedge if a hot PCE or a hawkish Warsh pushes the 10Y above 4.75%. I'm wrong if tankers start moving or talks restart: the war premium would drain out of WTI fast, and with RSI at 71.7 on light volume, XLE would fall back below its $63.46 breakout level.
- **Materials**: This is a bet on the weak dollar and on metals, not on factory demand: the 56.0 PMI was driven by services, while manufacturing fell to a five-month low. The drivers are the dollar below 99, the flight into gold (up 5.6% to $4,624 after Treasury doubled its bond buybacks with the debt past $40T), Freeport's breakout on a forecast copper shortage, and steel tariff protection that survived when the Canada talks collapsed. XLB closed at a five-month high and led Friday's bounce. I'm wrong if a hawkish Warsh sends the dollar back above 100 and knocks Newmont (NEM) down from an RSI of 84.6, or if WTI goes above $90 and squeezes the chemical makers.
- **Consumer Staples**: XLP finished flat in a week when WMT, its third-largest holding, fell 9.8%, because money moved into KO (at a 52-week high), PEP, GIS and CPB rather than leaving the sector. With a beta of about 0.52 it is the book's cushion if NVDA disappoints or the week turns risk-off, and the weak dollar helps the multinationals, but it is my weakest pick. I'm wrong if a cool PCE, a strong NVDA report and a patient Warsh spark a rally that leaves defensives behind, if the 10Y goes above 4.85% and hits bond-like staples, or if WMT keeps sliding after its miss and pulls COST down with it.

Her five:

- **LLY** (Healthcare): LLY is about 12% of XLV; it hit a new 52-week high ($1,292.65) during the breakout, which came on the heaviest volume of 2026, and closed only about 3% below it, so it is the most direct way to own more money moving into healthcare.
- **VRTX** (Healthcare): It rose 8.4% with the sector to within 1.3% of its $555.32 52-week high, giving a profitable large-cap way into a biotech rally that ignored a 52-week-high 10Y, with no dated drug decision to derail it.
- **REGN** (Healthcare): The Aug 19 approval of Pasatru sent it to a record $847 and it closed within 1.6% of that, with the strongest 4- and 12-week gains of the large caps (+27% and +36%) at under 20x earnings, which makes it the 'run of FDA approvals' part of my thesis.
- **COP** (Energy): The largest oil producer on my sheet hit a fresh 52-week high Friday as leadership moved from refiners to producers, so it gains most from a blockade confirmed by tanker counts, and it is the book's hedge if PCE or Warsh pushes the 10Y above 4.75%.
- **NUE** (Materials): The Canada-deal scare that cost it 9.4% ended Saturday with the 50% duties in force and steel prices at $1,181 a ton, so the drop was a policy scare, not damage to the business, and it should keep recovering toward and past its 50-day average ($248) after Monday's opening bounce.

Her summary: Healthcare is my strongest call: its breakout came on the year's heaviest volume with 13 of the 14 names we track up, and it moves on its own news rather than on PCE, NVDA or Warsh, so it gets three leaders sitting just below their highs. COP and NUE carry the Energy and Materials calls through the least-stretched catalysts I have. I passed on GLD, NEM and FCX (overbought going into Warsh), on BTC (a +22.5% jump in one week that none of my sectors explain), and on Staples, my weakest call.

His summary: Five cheap, sound businesses kept clear of the synthesis's danger zones: no utilities, nothing in the AI/semis complex with NVDA reporting Wednesday, the healthcare call taken through its cheapest name (REGN), and two rate beneficiaries (PGR, JPM) as a partial hedge if a hawkish Jackson Hole or a hot PCE sends the 10-year toward 5%.
