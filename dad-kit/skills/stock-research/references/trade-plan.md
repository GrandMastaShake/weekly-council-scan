# Trade plan

For "should I buy / sell / add to X?" Do not answer yes or no. Walk him
through the plan card, give your honest read of each line, and let him
decide. If he cannot fill in the "proven wrong" line, the plan is not ready,
and saying so is the most useful thing you can do.

## The plan card (buying or adding)

| Line | His answer |
|---|---|
| Ticker and date | |
| Why, in one sentence (the thesis) | |
| Evidence: two or three facts, each dated and sourced | |
| What would prove me wrong (a closing price, or an event) | |
| Stop: price, and whether it is a real order or a mental note | |
| Target or review date | |
| Size: % of the portfolio, and dollars | |
| Dollars lost if the stop is hit | |
| Events before the review date (earnings, Fed, CPI, jobs) | |
| Overlap: which current holdings would be wrong for the same reason | |

Offer to add the card to the Trade Plans sheet of his investing journal.

## Sizing arithmetic (use code, show the inputs)

    dollars at risk  = (entry price - stop price) x shares
    risk as % of all = dollars at risk / total portfolio value

Use the limits in his investing profile (max loss per idea, max position
size). If he has not set them, ask him to pick numbers before sizing anything.
Many individual investors keep the loss at the stop on any one idea to about
1% to 2% of the whole portfolio, but it is his rule to set, not yours.

## Checks to run, and to say out loud

- **Events.** A big scheduled event before the review date? The Council's
  rule of thumb: no new position into a major scheduled report without a stop.
- **Same bet.** If two holdings would be proven wrong by the same price or
  the same event, they are one bet. Add their sizes together and look again.
- **Chasing.** If the stock is up sharply in the last week or two, ask what
  he would do if it gave half of that back in the first week he owns it.
- **Earnings soon?** See `earnings-check.md`. A smaller size is the normal
  answer to buying right before a report.

## Selling

- Ask: "Would you buy it today, at this price, with what you know now?" If
  no, the reason he is holding is probably the price he paid, which the
  market does not know about.
- Sell because the thesis broke or the "proven wrong" line was hit, not only
  because it is down. Hold because the thesis is intact, not only because it
  is up.
- Taxes, general rule only: held more than one year is usually taxed as a
  long-term gain, one year or less as short-term (higher). Selling at a loss
  and buying the same thing back within 30 days before or after can trigger
  the wash sale rule. Suggest he confirm anything that matters with his tax
  preparer.

## Resting limit orders

If he places a limit order below the market, log it in the Limit Orders
sheet. Each weekly review he either re-confirms it (count goes up by one),
re-prices it, or cancels it. At three re-confirmations with no fill, ask him
plainly: "Do you want this stock at today's price, or not?"
