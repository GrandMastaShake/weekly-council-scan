# Communications

## Rules for anything sent to people

- Short. Most people read the first two lines on a phone.
- Day of the week and the date together: "Saturday, May 16". Check each pair
  with code before handing it over.
- One clear thing to do, and by when: reply, sign up, show up, pay.
- Who to contact, if the message needs one. Only names and numbers he has
  given you; otherwise write [contact TBD].
- Friendly and plain. No exclamation marks on bad news.
- Give him a subject line for emails, and a shorter version for a text or a
  sign when it would help.

## Templates (fill the brackets; ask for anything missing)

**Course closure or frost delay**
Subject: [Course] -- [closed / delayed start] [Day, Date]
Body: what is happening, until when (or "we will update by [time]"), why in
one sentence, what happens to tee times, who to call.

**Aeration notice** (send 2 to 3 weeks ahead, remind 3 days ahead)
What and when, which holes or areas, how long the greens take to recover,
whether rates are reduced, why it matters (healthier greens for the rest of
the season).

**Tournament announcement**
Name, date and time, format, start type, entry fee and what it includes,
how to register and the deadline, the cause if it is a fundraiser, who to
contact.

**Volunteer call**
What the event is, the jobs needed with times, what volunteers get (lunch,
a round, a shirt), how to sign up, the deadline.

**Reminder** (players or volunteers, 2 to 3 days before)
Date, arrival time, where to go first, what to bring, weather plan.

**Thank-you** (sponsors, volunteers, players)
Specific: name what they did or gave and what it made possible (money
raised, players hosted). Short. Send within a few days.

**Sign text**
Under 10 words where possible. Large type. For example: "Greens aerated
today -- thank you for your patience."

**Newsletter blurb**
Three to five sentences: what is coming, what just happened, one thank-you.

## Before sending

Re-read for: every date paired with the right weekday, every time with AM or
PM, every name spelled the way he spelled it, every price matching what he
gave you.
