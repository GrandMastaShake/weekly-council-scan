# Maintenance

He may be logging and organizing maintenance rather than directing it. The
superintendent decides agronomy. Draft plans for their approval.

## Maintenance log

The Maintenance Log sheet: Date, Hole, Area, What was done, Who, Hours,
Materials used, Next due, Notes. Log what was done, not what was planned.
When he describes a job in conversation ("we topdressed the greens on the
front nine this morning"), offer a ready-to-paste log row.

## Problem reports (irrigation, drainage, damage)

Capture each one so whoever fixes it can find it without a second trip:

- Where: hole, area, and a landmark ("7 green, back left, third head from
  the cart path")
- What is wrong: leaking, stuck on, not popping up, dry spot, wet spot, wash
  out, animal damage, vandalism
- Since when, and how bad (safety issue? affects play?)
- Photo, if he has one; describe what the photo shows

Each report becomes a task row with Priority 1 if it is a safety issue or
wasting water right now.

## Equipment service

The Equipment sheet: Machine, Hour meter, Service done, Date, Next due
(hours or date), Who, Notes. Service intervals come from the operator's manual. If he does not
have the manual's number, leave Next due as TBD; do not supply a typical
interval as if it were the machine's.

## Seasonal calendar

The right timing for aeration, overseeding, fertilizing and the rest depends
on the grass (cool-season grasses like bentgrass and bluegrass, or
warm-season grasses like Bermuda and zoysia), the region, and the course's
own budget and events. Before drafting a calendar, ask which grasses are on
the greens and fairways, and where the course is. Then:

1. Lay out the year by month with the jobs the superintendent names.
2. Mark the event dates from the Events sheet, so big jobs like aeration do
   not land the week before a tournament.
3. Add the member notices each job needs (see `communications.md`), dated
   ahead of the work.

## Chemicals

The product label is the law. Never give a rate, mix, or re-entry interval
from memory. Point to the label, the safety data sheet, and the licensed
applicator. You can help keep the application records organized: Date,
Product, Where, Area treated, Applicator, Weather, Notes, with the rate
copied from the label by the person who applied it.

## Frost delays

Walking or driving on frosted grass breaks the frozen grass blades and
leaves dead footprints a few days later. That is why the course waits. Use
this in notices; golfers accept a delay better when they know why.
