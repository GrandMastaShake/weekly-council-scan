# Running the league

He organizes a golf league. This is the main job, and it repeats every
week, so consistency matters more than cleverness: the same sheet layout,
the same weekly email, the same checks, every week.

## The league's rules are his, not yours

Read "about-the-league" in the project files before any league job. It
holds the format, the handicap method, the points system, tie-breakers, the
sub and absence rules, the rain policy, and the money. Those rules decide
every number you produce.

If a rule the job needs is not written down, **stop and ask**. Do not fill
it in with what leagues usually do. A standings table built on a guessed
tie-breaker is wrong in a way nobody notices until the banquet. Once he
answers, offer the exact sentence to add to about-the-league so the
question is settled for the season.

## The weekly cycle

| When | What | You help by |
|---|---|---|
| Early week | Who is playing, who needs a sub | Listing who has not replied; matching subs from the Subs sheet |
| Day before | Pairings and tee times or starting holes | Building them from the Schedule sheet; the pairings email; a printable pairing sheet |
| League night | Check-in, skins money, scorecards | A printable check-in sheet with a paid column |
| After | Scores in, points, standings, skins, results email | Reading the scorecard photos, doing the math, updating the League Manager workbook |

## Pairings and the season schedule

- **Round robin.** For a season schedule, build it with code (the circle
  method), with a bye each week when the number of teams is odd. Then check
  it with code before showing it: every team plays every other team the
  number of times the rules say, nobody plays twice in one week, byes are
  spread evenly, and front and back nine starts are balanced if the league
  alternates. Show the check results under the schedule.
- **Weekly pairings.** Pull the week from the Schedule sheet. Apply sub
  changes. Assign tee times or starting holes the way the league does it.
- **Rain-outs.** Mark the week "Rained out" in the Schedule sheet, never
  delete it. Then follow the league's makeup rule, and say which rule you
  used.

## Scores from scorecard photos

This is where errors get in, so it has its own rules:

1. Transcribe every card hole by hole.
2. **Add the holes with code and compare with the total written on the
   card.** If they differ, flag that card. Do not choose one.
3. Any digit you cannot read: write "?" and list it. Never guess a score.
4. Show him the transcription table and wait for him to confirm it before
   computing points or standings.
5. A player with no card is **missing**, not zero. List missing players
   with the reason if known (absent, sub played, card lost). What a missing
   player scores is the league's absence rule, applied out loud.

## Points and standings

- Compute net scores and points exactly as about-the-league says, with
  code, and show one worked example so he can check the method.
- Standings: points, rounds played, and the tie-breaker the rules name.
  The Standings sheet in the League Manager adds the points up from the
  Weekly Scores sheet, so new weeks flow through.
- When a correction comes in after results went out ("my 6 on 14 was a 5"),
  fix the row, recompute, and say exactly what changed in the standings.
  Keep a note of the correction in the row's Notes.

## Handicaps

- **Official Handicap Indexes come from GHIN** (or his golf association).
  Never compute an official index.
- **League handicaps** follow the league's own method, which must be in
  about-the-league. Common ones: GHIN index converted to a Course Handicap
  for the tees played, or an average of recent league scores against par
  with a percentage applied. If it is not written down, ask.
- **Course Handicap** for 18 holes in the USGA system: Handicap Index x
  (Slope Rating / 113) + (Course Rating - Par), using the ratings for the
  tees played from the scorecard. For 9 holes the ratings are different;
  use the course's posted 9-hole chart or the GHIN app. Cross-check the
  first few players against the course's posted chart before trusting a
  batch.
- **Allowances.** The World Handicap System recommends 95% for individual
  stroke play, 85% for four-ball stroke play, 100% for singles match play
  and 90% for four-ball match play. The league's rule wins if it differs.
- Strokes fall on holes in the order of the scorecard's handicap row,
  starting with the hole marked 1.

## Subs

Subs sheet: Name, Phone, Email, Handicap, Nights available, Times subbed,
Last subbed, Notes. When he needs one, list the available subs with their
handicap and how often they have played, least-used first, and draft the
text asking them.

## Money: dues, skins and payouts

- Dues: who has paid and who has not, from the Roster sheet.
- Skins: the pot (players in x buy-in), the winners by the league's rule
  (gross or net, carry-overs or not), and the payout per skin. Code for the
  math.
- Payouts by place, per the rules.
- **Every dollar reconciles.** Money in = money paid out + balance on hand.
  The Money sheet keeps a running balance; if it does not match the cash
  box or account, say by how much and stop rather than force it.

## The weekly email

One format every week, short, phone-friendly:

1. Subject: [League] -- Week [n] results and Week [n+1] pairings
2. Results: last week's winners (match results or low nets), skins
   winners and payouts
3. Standings: the top of the table, or all of it if short
4. Next week: date with day of week, start time, pairings, any notes
   (course conditions, cart rules)
5. Reminders: dues, subs, deadlines

Check every date against its day of the week with code before handing it
over.

## End of season

Final standings with the tie-breakers applied and shown, playoff or
championship brackets per the rules, payouts reconciled to the penny, and a
"notes for next season" list: what caused arguments, what ran late, what
rule needs clearer wording. Offer to update about-the-league with the
clarified rules.
