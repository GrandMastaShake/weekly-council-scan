# Task lists and meeting notes

## From a brain dump, a list, or a photo

1. Pull out every task, one per row, in the standard columns: ID, Task,
   Hole, Area, Who, Priority, Due, Status, Done on, Notes.
2. Where a detail is missing, leave it blank or TBD. Do not guess who does
   what.
3. Merge duplicates ("fix sprinkler on 7" and "7 green head leaking" are
   probably one job; ask if unsure).
4. Sort: Priority 1 first, then by due date.
5. Offer a **route order** for jobs out on the course: group them by hole in
   playing order, so one cart trip covers them. Clubhouse and shop jobs go
   in their own group.
6. Finish with "Still needed from you": the blanks that matter, as a short
   numbered list of questions.

From a photo of a whiteboard or handwritten notes: transcribe first, then
list anything you could not read as "unreadable: [best guess?]" rather than
guessing silently.

## Weekly plan

When he asks "what's on for this week?": Priority 1 and 2 items that are not
Done, grouped by day if they have due dates, then a short "if there's time"
list from Priority 3. Flag anything Waiting for more than a week and say who
it is waiting on.

## Recurring jobs

Jobs that repeat (weekly, monthly, seasonal) go in the list once with
"Repeats: weekly" in Notes. When one is marked Done, add the next one with
its new due date.

## Meeting notes to action items

1. **Decisions:** what was decided, one line each.
2. **Action items:** the standard task columns. Every action item needs a
   Who and a Due. If the notes do not say, mark it TBD and put it under
   "Still needed".
3. **Open questions:** what was raised but not decided, and who owns the
   answer.
4. **Follow-up message:** a short email or text recapping decisions and
   action items, ready to send. Names and dates only if they are in the
   notes.

## Status update

When he needs to tell someone where things stand: three short sections,
Done (since last update), In progress, Need help / waiting on. Keep it to
what the reader needs, not everything that happened.
