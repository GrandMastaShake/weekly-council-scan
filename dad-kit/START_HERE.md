# Welcome to Claude, Dad

This is your setup, and a short guide to getting the most out of it.

## What Claude is good at

Think of Claude as a sharp assistant who has read a great deal, writes well,
never gets tired of questions, and can build spreadsheets and documents on
the spot. It is good at:

- Explaining things: a company, an earnings report, a market term, a rule
- Organizing: a pile of notes into a to-do list, a schedule, a plan
- Writing: emails, notices, sign-up sheets, thank-you notes
- Reading what you give it: a statement, a PDF, a photo of a whiteboard or a
  handwritten list
- Doing the math, and showing its work

## What to watch for

- **It can be wrong, and sound sure.** If a number matters, ask: "Where did
  that number come from, and what date is it from?" Your setup tells Claude
  to give sources and dates for market numbers without being asked.
- **It does not see live prices on its own.** It searches the web when it
  needs to. Check any price yourself before you place an order.
- **It is not your broker.** It helps you think. You decide, and you click
  the button.
- **Never give it** passwords, account numbers, or your Social Security
  number. It does not need them.

## Your setup

You have two **Projects**. A project is a room with its own instructions and
files, so Claude already knows the background when you walk in.

- **Stocks** -- knows your goals and your limits, follows the family
  Council's rules for numbers, and can read the Council's weekly brief.
- **Golf Course** -- knows the course, who's who, and how you like things
  laid out.

Start a chat **inside** the right project. A chat started outside a project
works fine, it just does not have the background.

You also have two **Skills**, which run in the background. You never have to
call them; Claude uses them when the topic comes up.

And two spreadsheets: the **Investing Journal** and the **Golf Course
Organizer**. Keep them on your computer. When you want one updated, attach it
to a chat and say what changed. Claude hands back the whole updated file;
save it over the old one.

## How to talk to it

- Talk the way you would to a capable assistant. Full sentences, plenty of
  background. More context gets a better answer.
- It is a conversation. If the first answer misses, say so: "shorter",
  "make it a table", "that's not what I meant, I meant ...", "put this in a
  spreadsheet I can print".
- If you are not sure how to ask, say: "Ask me questions until you
  understand what I need."
- On your phone you can talk instead of type (the microphone button), and
  take a photo of anything and ask about it.

## Things to try first

**In the Stocks project**

1. "What did the Council say this week? Give me the Dad Translation."
2. "Give me a stock brief on [a stock you own]."
3. "Here's my statement [attach it]. How does my portfolio look?"
4. "I'm thinking about buying [stock]. Help me write a plan."
5. "[Company] reports earnings next week. What should I watch for?"
6. "Let's do my weekly review." (attach your Investing Journal)

**In the Golf Course project**

1. "Here's everything on my plate this week: ... Turn it into a list."
2. [photo of a whiteboard] "Turn this into a task list."
3. "We're running a scramble on [date] for about [number] players. Build me
   the planning checklist."
4. "Here are the volunteers and when they can work: ... Build a schedule for
   [dates]."
5. "Write a notice to members that the greens will be aerated [dates]."
6. "Here are my notes from the committee meeting: ... What did we decide,
   and who's doing what?"

## When you get stuck

Ask Claude: "How do I [do the thing] in Claude?" It can explain its own
settings. Or text your favorite child.
