> *Auto-updated by the Synthesis Agent every Saturday. Data as of Friday Oct 16 close. This edition was written Sunday evening Oct 18 because the Saturday jobs ran late; every research page it uses was finished and up to date before it started.*

### The Headline

The AI trade had its worst week in a year and a half. Tech stocks fell **7.7%** and chip stocks **10.5%**, the chip index fell eight days in a row, its longest losing streak since 2008, and the S&P 500 lost **2.0%**, its worst week since June, after September inflation came in hotter than expected (**3.7%** a year) and Broadcom's CEO said many AI investors are acting like they are "in a casino." The counter-story is where the money went: ten of our twelve sectors beat the market, grocery-aisle staples rose **5.0%**, oil companies **4.9%** with crude above **$100** for the first time this fall, and healthcare hit a one-year high, while long-term interest rates *fell* to **5.098%** as investors ran to bonds. The VIX fear gauge jumped **35%** to **19.99**, and touched **22.31** on Friday on an unconfirmed rumor that a big AI-focused hedge fund had blown up. The corporate bombshell is a strange one: TSMC and ASML, the two most important AI chip suppliers, both reported great quarters and raised their outlooks, and their stocks fell 9% and 8% anyway.

### The Bottom Line

The Council's own book had a rough week (about **-5.3%** by Friday's closes against **-2.0%** for the S&P, provisional until Monday's official scoring), and three of its five names closed below their practice stop-loss levels. On cash, the research desks want **25–30%** while the Council's book is capped at **20%**; the rest of the protection comes from hedges, which are no longer cheap with the VIX near 20 but are cheaper than they will be if Friday's rumor turns out to be real. No new interest-rate bets without a stop before Wednesday's auction, and no new AI purchases before Meta.

## How It Works

Every **Monday at 9:03 AM** (America/Indianapolis), the Council convenes:

1. **Cecil** (Fundamentalist) scores stocks on P/E ratios, earnings quality, and safety
2. **Marky** (Technician) reads momentum, moving averages, and volatility
3. **Ophelia** (Macroeconomist) tracks sector rotation, market regime, and risk-adjusted returns
4. **Consensus** weights each agent by historical accuracy, converges on a Top 5 portfolio
