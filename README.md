## 🌡️ Market Brief — Week of 2026-09-13

> *Auto-updated by the Synthesis Agent every Saturday. Data as of Friday Sep 11 close.*

### The Headline

The market spent four sessions pricing the Federal Reserve's first rate hike of the cycle entirely by itself, and then rallied anyway. August core inflation ran hotter than expected (+0.3% against +0.2% forecast), September hike odds vaulted from roughly 50% to **87–90%** in five sessions, and the 10-year Treasury closed at **4.975%** — a fresh 52-week high, just 2.5 basis points under the 5.00% line the Council treats as a crisis threshold. The counter-story is that the worst case never showed: annual core inflation actually *fell* to 2.4%, the lowest since March 2021, and Friday's relief rally added 509 points to the Dow. The VIX briefly flinched — spiking to 17.84 Wednesday, its first genuine crack in months — then gave it all back to close at **15.84**, still historically cheap insurance. The corporate bombshell was Oracle: a blowout quarter with revenue up 30%, a 10% earnings beat, a raised full-year outlook, and a record **$664 billion** contracted backlog — and the stock gapped up 7–8% at the open and **closed red**, because the same release guided capital spending to $90–95 billion. When a beat-and-raise like that gets sold the same morning, the discount rate is running the tape.
