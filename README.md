## 🌡️ Market Brief — Week of 2026-10-04

> *Auto-updated by the Synthesis Agent every Saturday. ### The Headline

The Federal Reserve backed away from an October rate hike, and long-term interest rates went up anyway. The economy added only **29,000 jobs** in September (about 85,000–90,000 were expected), a key inflation reading cooled, and two senior Fed officials said there was "no need for urgency," so the odds of an October hike fell from roughly two-in-three to about **one-in-five**. But the 10-year Treasury yield still rose to **5.277%** and touched 5.34% on Thursday, the highest since 2002, because the people who lend to the government for long periods keep asking for more. Stocks split in two: chipmakers (+4.0%) and big tech (+1.8%) hit records while eight of our twelve sectors fell, and the S&P 500 ended almost flat (**-0.2%**). The counter-story is stress underneath: consumer confidence dropped to its lowest since 2014, mortgage rates jumped to 7.28%, and the extra interest that lower-rated companies pay to borrow rose every day for eight sessions, to its highest since March. The VIX closed at **15.31** after spending four days above 16. The corporate surprise: Micron reported a record quarter with an 87% profit margin, and its stock finished the week *lower*, while the companies that sell Micron its factory equipment jumped 10–11%. On Sunday, OPEC+ left its oil output targets unchanged for November, as expected.

## How It Works

Every **Monday at 9:03 AM** (America/Indianapolis), the Council convenes:

1. **Cecil** (Fundamentalist) scores stocks on P/E ratios, earnings quality, and safety
2. **Marky** (Technician) reads momentum, moving averages, and volatility
3. **Ophelia** (Macroeconomist) tracks sector rotation, market regime, and risk-adjusted returns
4. **Consensus** weights each agent by historical accuracy, converges on a Top 5 portfolio
