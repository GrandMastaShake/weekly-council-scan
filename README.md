## 🌡️ Market Brief — Week of 2026-10-12

> *Auto-updated by the Synthesis Agent every Saturday. Data as of Friday Oct 9 close. This edition was written early Monday Oct 12 because every Saturday job ran late; it waited for the Canary Watch data refresh before using any macro number.*

### The Headline

The stock market set a new record this week, and it did it without the chipmakers. The S&P 500 closed at a record on Tuesday and ended the week up **1.2%**, while the semiconductor group fell **4.3%** after two reports on Thursday raised the same uncomfortable question: the Financial Times said OpenAI's revenue is running about $20 billion below what people assumed, and the Wall Street Journal said Broadcom is trying to raise more than $50 billion so OpenAI can buy Broadcom's chips. Money left the chip stocks and went almost everywhere else: eight of our twelve sectors rose, led by utilities (+4.0%), energy (+3.6%), grocery-aisle staples (+3.6%) and healthcare (+2.8%), ending losing streaks of four to eight weeks in four of them. The counter-story is that the economy's mood got worse while stocks improved: consumer sentiment fell to **46.3** with its "how are things right now" gauge at an all-time low, mortgage rates rose to **7.40%**, PepsiCo cut its profit forecast and Delta cut its full-year forecast by a fifth. Long-term interest rates touched their highest level since 2002 on Wednesday (10-year at 5.36%) and then eased to **5.244%** by Friday after two big government bond sales went fine, and the VIX fear gauge fell to **14.84**. The corporate bombshells came in pairs: SpaceX agreed to buy about $8 billion of wireless spectrum, and on Friday T-Mobile fell 13%, AT&T 10% and Verizon 9% while cell-tower landlords jumped 12–20%; Google signed Constellation Energy to a 20-year nuclear power deal and Constellation and Vistra each rose 15%.

### The Bottom Line

The regime is **a Fed that says it will hike again (December, not October), a job market that has stopped hiring, and an oil shock that hasn't reached core inflation yet**. The rotation story is real this time: money left the chip stocks on a question about who funds the AI buildout and spread into eight other sectors, ending four long losing streaks and lifting the number of sectors above their long-term trend to six of twelve. But each new leader rests on one day or one headline, so the Council isn't chasing any of them: utilities on a Google contract, energy on a presidential post, towers on a tenant that hasn't signed, payments on a move nobody can explain. **The single most important thing is Wednesday at 8:30 AM**, when September's inflation report lands: a hot core number hits exactly the sectors that just started working and sends long-term rates back toward their highs, while a tame one clears one of the Council's three warning flags. The bank reports Tuesday are the first word on whether 5.2% long-term rates have started to hurt borrowers; high-yield spreads came off their peak this week but are still above 3 points. The Council keeps its winners with stops, is out of its chip momentum position until the $609.66 line is recovered, adds nothing that needs falling rates before the CPI, and keeps the dollar restriction on commodity buying. On cash, the research desks want **25–30%** while the Council's own book is capped at **20%**, so the rest of the protection comes from hedges, which are still cheap with the VIX at **14.84**.

## How It Works

Every **Monday at 9:03 AM** (America/Indianapolis), the Council convenes:

1. **Cecil** (Fundamentalist) scores stocks on P/E ratios, earnings quality, and safety
2. **Marky** (Technician) reads momentum, moving averages, and volatility
3. **Ophelia** (Macroeconomist) tracks sector rotation, market regime, and risk-adjusted returns
4. **Consensus** weights each agent by historical accuracy, converges on a Top 5 portfolio
